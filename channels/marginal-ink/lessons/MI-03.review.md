# Fact-check — MI-03

Lesson MI-03 reads opportunity cost off the production possibilities frontier
(PPF): the PPF as the boundary of attainable output, points on it (efficient),
inside (wasteful) and outside (unattainable); opportunity cost as the magnitude
of the PPF slope measured between two points (rise over run); and the law of
increasing opportunity cost, which gives the frontier its outward-bending
(concave) shape. Source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, §2.2
(pp. 34-38), CC BY 4.0.

**Notes on scope.**

- **All numbers are our own.** §2.2 is explicit that the PPF carries no numbers:

  > "The second major difference is the absence of specific numbers on the axes
  > of the PPF. There are no specific numbers because we do not know the exact
  > amount of resources this imaginary economy has ..." (§2.2, p. 35)

  So nothing in §2.2 can be copied verbatim — every coordinate (A, B, C, D) and
  every opportunity-cost value in this lesson is invented. The lesson places
  them on a smooth concave curve `y = 10 − 0.1x²` (the `ppf` plot function) with
  gentle slope near the top (A, B) and steep slope lower down (C, D), exactly the
  shape §2.2 describes.

- **`effNote` and `incEq` are not in the `equations:` block.** `effNote` (scene
  s2) is a `text` item with no `latex`, so the gate does not scaffold it; `incEq`
  (scene s3) is a `latex` comparison `slope_{A→B} < slope_{C→D}`, which the gate
  scaffolded only under the two-point slopes it feeds (`ocEq` / the problem
  steps). Both are checked in prose below — `effNote` under "Scene s2", `incEq`
  under "Scene s3" — since both restate §2.2 directly.

## Scene s2 — the PPF as boundary (`drawPPF`, `markP/Q/R`, `effNote`)

The frontier is the set of attainable combinations when all resources are used,
and it separates the possible from the impossible:

> "Society can choose any combination of the two goods on or inside the PPF.
> However, it does not have enough resources to produce outside the PPF." (§2.2,
> p. 34)

**On the frontier = efficient** (`markP`, green):

> "Productive efficiency means that, given the available inputs and technology,
> it is impossible to produce more of one good without decreasing the quantity
> that is produced of another good. All choices on the PPF ... display
> productive efficiency." (§2.2, p. 36)

**Inside = wasteful** (`markQ`, red):

> "any choice inside the production possibilities frontier is productively
> inefficient and wasteful because it is possible to produce more of one good,
> the other good, or some combination of both goods." (§2.2, p. 36)

> "point R is productively inefficient because it is possible at choice C to have
> more of both goods" (§2.2, p. 36)

**Outside = unattainable** (`markR`, yellow):

> "it does not have enough resources to produce outside the PPF." (§2.2, p. 34)

`effNote` ("On the frontier = efficient; inside = wasteful; outside =
unattainable.") is the one-line summary of the three quotes above. The
narration's "scarcity made visible ... producing more of one good always means
producing less of the other" matches:

> "Because the PPF is downward sloping from left to right, the only way society
> can obtain more education is by giving up some healthcare. That is the tradeoff
> society faces." (§2.2, p. 34)

`ok` — boundary, efficient/inside/outside all quoted from §2.2.

## Equation `ocEq` — opportunity cost = |ΔY/ΔX| = (Y₁−Y₂)/(X₂−X₁)

Opportunity cost is the magnitude of the PPF slope, read as rise over run
between two points:

> "the slope of the production possibilities frontier shows the opportunity
> cost." (§2.2, p. 34)

> "The slope of the PPF between B and C is (approximately) the vertical distance
> (the 'rise') over the horizontal distance (the 'run'). This is the opportunity
> cost of the additional education." (§2.2, p. 35)

> "The opportunity cost would be the healthcare society has to forgo." (§2.2,
> p. 33-34)

Our `ocEq` writes this as the healthcare given up (ΔY, the rise) over the
education gained (ΔX, the run), as an absolute value because the frontier slopes
down. `ok`.

## Scene s3 — increasing cost and the curved shape (`incEq`)

`incEq` (`slope_{A→B} < slope_{C→D}`): the slope is gentle near the top and steep
lower down, so the cost rises as education expands:

> "the shape of the PPF from A to B is relatively flat, representing a relatively
> small drop-off in health and a relatively large gain in education." (§2.2,
> p. 35)

> "the slope of the PPF between D and F is steep, showing a large drop in health
> for only a small gain in education." (§2.2, p. 35)

This is named and tied to the outward bend:

> "the law of increasing opportunity cost, which holds that as production of a
> good or service increases, the marginal opportunity cost of producing it
> increases as well. This happens because some resources are better suited for
> producing certain goods and services instead of others." (§2.2, p. 35)

> "In this way, the law of increasing opportunity cost produces the outward-
> bending shape of the production possibilities frontier." (§2.2, p. 35)

The narration's "if every resource were equally good at both goods ... the
frontier would be a straight line" is the contrast §2.2 draws with the (straight)
budget constraint, whose slope is constant; the PPF "has a curved shape because
of the law of the diminishing returns" (§2.2, p. 35). `ok`.

## Problem `s5` — opportunity cost on the steep part

```python
# s5: C=(7,5.1), D=(9,1.9)
dX = 9 - 7
dY = 5.1 - 1.9
oc = (5.1 - 1.9) / (9 - 7)
print("dX =", dX, " dY =", round(dY, 10), " opp cost =", oc)
print("check expr (5.1 - 1.9)/(9 - 7) =", (5.1 - 1.9)/(9 - 7))
ppf = lambda x: 10 - 0.1*x*x
print("ppf(7) =", round(ppf(7),10), " ppf(9) =", round(ppf(9),10))
# increasing-cost comparison and concavity (y'' = -0.2 < 0 everywhere)
print("rising:", oc, ">", 0.4, "->", oc > 0.4, " ratio =", round(oc/0.4, 6))
print("y'' = -0.2 < 0  -> concave / outward-bending")
```
