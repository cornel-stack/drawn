# Fact-check — MA-24

Lesson MA-24 builds the Solow growth model, the convergence hypothesis, the
empirical puzzle (absolute convergence often fails / Lucas paradox) and its
resolution (conditional convergence). The **economics** — diminishing returns
to capital, capital deepening, convergence as a consequence of diminishing
returns, and productivity/technology (not capital alone) driving long-run
growth — is squarely in OpenStax §20.3 and §20.4. The **formal algebra** of the
steady-state condition and the conditional-convergence parameterisation is the
standard Solow/neoclassical growth construction, which intro OpenStax presents
in words and figures rather than equations. This is flagged in the lesson header.

**No incorrect economic claim found.** The source §20.4 directly asserts the
chain the lesson teaches: diminishing returns ⇒ capital-scarce (poor) countries
have a larger marginal effect from capital deepening ⇒ they *could* converge;
but convergence is not inevitable because technology and supportive
institutions determine whether it actually happens.

## Equation `steadyEq`

`s f(k*) = (n+δ) k*` — the steady state where investment just replaces
worn-out and population-diluted capital.

> "Where investment meets this replacement line, the economy reaches its steady
> state" — board framing of §20. OpenStax describes the per-capita production
> function and its diminishing returns, and that growth from capital deepening
> "must necessarily slow down" without technology (§20.4, p.494).

NOTE: standard Solow growth-model / convergence construction; intro OpenStax
covers diminishing returns, capital deepening and productivity in words (quotes
provided), the formal steady-state and convergence algebra is the standard
construction, flagged in the lesson header.

## Equation `returnsEq`

`MPK = f'(k) ↓ as k ↑` — the marginal product of capital falls as capital rises.
Directly stated by OpenStax:

> "the law of diminishing returns suggests that as an economy continues to
> increase its human and physical capital, the marginal gains to economic
> growth will diminish." — §20.4 Economic Convergence, p.493.

> "the way in which the line starts out steeper on the left but then flattens as
> it moves to the right shows the diminishing marginal returns, as additional
> marginal amounts of capital deepening increase output by ever-smaller
> amounts." — §20.4, Figure 20.7 discussion, p.494.

## Equation `conditionalEq`

`k* = k*(s, n, A)` — each country converges to its own steady state, set by its
saving rate, population growth and productivity/institutions.

> "When it comes to adapting and using new technology, a society's performance
> is not necessarily guaranteed, but is the result of whether the country's
> economic, educational, and public policy institutions are supportive... if
> they lack the appropriate supportive economic infrastructure and institutions,
> the theoretical possibility that backwardness might have certain advantages is
> of little practical relevance." — §20.4, p.495.

NOTE: standard Solow conditional-convergence construction; OpenStax makes the
economic point (the target depends on technology and institutions, not just
capital) in words; the parameterisation `k*(s,n,A)` is the standard algebra,
flagged in the lesson header.

## Equation `step1`

`s√k = δk` — investment equals replacement at the steady state, with f(k)=√k and
(n+δ) collapsed to δ for the single-parameter example.

NOTE: standard Solow steady-state condition; see `steadyEq`.

## Equation `step2`

`√k = s/δ ⇒ k = (s/δ)²` — algebraic rearrangement of `step1`. Verified:

```python
import sympy as sp
k, s, d = sp.symbols('k s delta', positive=True)
sol = sp.solve(sp.Eq(s*sp.sqrt(k), d*k), k)
print(sol)                       # solve s√k = δk for k
print(sp.simplify(sol[0] - (s/d)**2) == 0)
```

output:
```
[s**2/delta**2]
True
```

So k* = (s/δ)², confirming step2.
