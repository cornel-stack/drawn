# Fact-check — MA-07

Lesson MA-07 teaches consumption smoothing: it builds the two-period model —
indifference curves over present vs future consumption and an intertemporal
budget line — finds the utility-maximising (tangency) choice, shows the consumer
prefers a flat consumption path and uses saving and borrowing to reach it, states
the Euler equation MRS = 1+r, and generalises to the permanent-income idea that
consumption tracks permanent rather than current income. Two worked problems
follow on our own illustrative figures (smoothing by saving at y1=100,y2=50;
smoothing by borrowing at y1=40,y2=80; r=0 throughout).

Qualitative source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, Appendix B
"Indifference Curves", section "Indifference Curves with Labor-Leisure and
Intertemporal Choices" (Intertemporal Choice / Quentin example), CC BY 4.0,
pp.865-867. Supporting consumer-choice material: §6.2 "How Changes in Income and
Prices Affect Consumption Choices" (utility-maximising choice on a budget
constraint).

## IMPORTANT distinction — what is OpenStax and what is our construction

This follows the OpenStax-vs-standard split established in MA-05/MA-06: the
OpenStax Principles treatment is **qualitative** (indifference curves and a
budget constraint, choices and tangencies described in words and figures), and
the **algebra is the standard intermediate two-period / permanent-income model**
that we layer on top. Nothing is copied; the worked numbers are our own.

**Supported qualitatively by OpenStax Appendix B (Intertemporal Choice)** (cited
with quotes + pages below):

- *Intertemporal choice represented with indifference curves — consumption today
  vs consumption in the future.* "The concept of an indifference curve applies to
  tradeoffs in any household choice, including the labor-leisure choice or the
  intertemporal choice between present and future consumption." (p.865 / source
  lines 39-40) "In an intertemporal choice, each indifference curve shows the
  combinations of present and future consumption that provide a certain level of
  utility." (p.867 / source lines 42-43). This grounds the two-period indifference
  map (`lowCurve`, `highCurve`) and the c1/c2 axes.
- *A budget constraint between present and future consumption, with saving and a
  rate of return moving consumption between periods.* "Figure B6 shows Quentin's
  budget constraint and his indifference curves between present consumption and
  future consumption." (p.867 / source lines 101-102) "...he is consuming $6,000,
  saving $4,000 for the future, and expecting with the accumulated interest to
  have $7,200 for future consumption (that is, $4,000 in current financial savings
  plus the 80% rate of return)." (p.867 / source lines 103-105). This grounds the
  budget line (`budget`, `budgetEq`), the rate of return r, and saving/borrowing
  as the way to reallocate consumption across periods.
- *The utility-maximising choice is a tangency of an indifference curve to the
  budget constraint.* "The highest level of utility that Quentin can achieve at
  his original intertemporal budget constraint occurs at point A..." (p.867 /
  source lines 101-103); generally the optimum is where the budget constraint is
  "tangent to the ... indifference curve" (labour-leisure, p.865-866 / source
  lines 48, 53-54, 70-72). This grounds the optimum (`optimum`, point E) and the
  tangency idea behind the Euler equation.
- *The budget line's slope is set by the rate of return / relative price of
  future consumption.* The substitution effect follows because "the lower rate of
  return makes future consumption relatively more expensive and present
  consumption relatively cheaper" (p.867 / source lines 114-115), i.e. r prices
  present against future consumption. This grounds the slope −(1+r). (Supporting:
  §6.2, the utility-maximising choice on a budget constraint, p.143-146.)

**STANDARD TWO-PERIOD / PERMANENT-INCOME CONSTRUCTION — NOT in OpenStax
Principles.** Flagged clearly and layered by us on the OpenStax qualitative
treatment above:

- the **algebraic intertemporal budget** c1 + c2/(1+r) = y1 + y2/(1+r), with the
  discount factor 1/(1+r) and present value of consumption = present value of
  income (`budgetEq`, `budgetEq2`);
- the **Euler equation** MRS_{c1,c2} = 1+r — the tangency condition written
  symbolically, and the consumption-growth reading (`eulerEq`);
- the **permanent-income hypothesis** c = permanent income (`permEq`), with the
  windfall-saved / permanent-raise-spent and liquidity-constraint commentary;
- all worked numbers (y1=100,y2=50 and y1=40,y2=80, r=0) and both problems.

OpenStax Appendix B describes intertemporal choice only with indifference curves,
a budget constraint and a rate of return in words and figures: it never writes
the present-value budget equation, never introduces the discount factor 1/(1+r)
algebraically, never writes a tangency/Euler condition MRS = 1+r, and never
states a permanent-income hypothesis. The equation, the Euler condition, the
permanent-income statement and all numbers are our own standard-model
construction.

## Equation `budgetEq` — `c1 + c2/(1+r) = y1 + y2/(1+r)`

STANDARD TWO-PERIOD-MODEL CONSTRUCTION (the present-value intertemporal budget).
The qualitative object — a budget constraint between present and future
consumption, with saving at a rate of return — is the source:

> "In an intertemporal choice, each indifference curve shows the combinations of
> present and future consumption that provide a certain level of utility." (App.
> B, Intertemporal Choice, p.867; source lines 42-43)

> "Figure B6 shows Quentin's budget constraint and his indifference curves
> between present consumption and future consumption. ... he is consuming $6,000,
> saving $4,000 for the future, and expecting with the accumulated interest to
> have $7,200 for future consumption (that is, $4,000 in current financial
> savings plus the 80% rate of return)." (App. B, p.867; source lines 101-105)

The symbolic present-value budget line, the discount factor 1/(1+r), and the
separation of income (y1,y2) from consumption (c1,c2) are OUR construction. `ok`.

## Equation `budgetEq2` — `c1 + c2/(1+r) = y1 + y2/(1+r)`

STANDARD TWO-PERIOD-MODEL CONSTRUCTION (identical budget restated in the
derivation; not in OpenStax). Same qualitative support as `budgetEq` (App. B
p.867, source lines 42-43, 101-105). The s3 narration — "a pound next period is
worth only one over one plus r today, because lending it would earn interest" and
the slope "minus one plus r" — is the standard present-value reading of the
source's rate of return r (Quentin earns a return over the periods, p.867 /
source lines 101-107). OUR construction. `ok`.

## Equation `eulerEq` — `MRS_{c1,c2} = 1 + r`

STANDARD TWO-PERIOD-MODEL CONSTRUCTION (the Euler equation; not in OpenStax). The
tangency idea it encodes is exactly the source's utility-maximising choice:

> "The highest level of utility that Quentin can achieve at his original
> intertemporal budget constraint occurs at point A, where he is consuming
> $6,000, saving $4,000 for the future..." (App. B, Intertemporal Choice, p.867;
> source lines 101-103)

and generally the optimum is a tangency of an indifference curve to the budget
constraint (labour-leisure example):

> "...the highest level of utility for Petunia, on her original budget
> constraint, is at choice A, where it is tangent to the lower indifference curve
> (Ul)." (App. B, p.865-866; source lines 48-51)

Writing that tangency as MRS = 1+r, the name "Euler equation", and the
consumption-growth reading (consumption grows only if r rewards waiting; c1=c2
when r just offsets impatience) are OUR construction. `ok`.

## Equation `permEq` — `c = permanent income`

STANDARD PERMANENT-INCOME-HYPOTHESIS CONSTRUCTION (not in OpenStax). The source's
qualitative backing is the intertemporal map itself — indifference curves over
present vs future consumption, with saving and borrowing reallocating consumption
across periods (App. B, Intertemporal Choice, p.867; source lines 42-43,
101-105). The statement that a household spends on permanent (lifetime-wealth-
sustainable) rather than current income, the windfall-mostly-saved /
permanent-raise-fully-spent predictions, and the liquidity-constraint diagnostic
are standard macro theory, not source text. OUR construction. `ok`.

## Problem `s4` — smoothing by saving (y1=100, y2=50, r=0)

```python
y1, y2, r = 100, 50, 0
smooth = (y1 + y2) / 2      # Euler MRS=1 at r=0 => c1=c2 => equal split
saving = y1 - smooth        # consume 75 while earning 100
print("smooth c       =", smooth)
print("saving y1 - c  =", saving)
print("s4 check expr (100 + 50) / 2 =", (100 + 50) / 2)
```

output:
```
smooth c       = 75.0
saving y1 - c  = 25.0
s4 check expr (100 + 50) / 2 = 75.0
```

**Verdict `ok`.**
- `step1`: c1 = c2 = (100+50)/2 = 75 — exact.
- `answer`: c = 75, save = 100 − 75 = 25 — exact.
- `check.expr` `(100 + 50) / 2` = 75.0 (value 75, tol 1%) — matches.

## Diagram `s2` — the intertemporal optimum (perfect smoothing)

The s2 diagram runs the `indifference` primitive with Cobb-Douglas utility
U = c1^a · c2^b, a=b=1, and budget px·c1 + py·c2 = m with px=1, py=1, m=12. The
Cobb-Douglas optimum is c1 = a/(a+b)·m/px, c2 = b/(a+b)·m/py, which with equal
weights and equal prices must land on the 45-degree line c1=c2 (perfect
smoothing), matching the lesson's claim that the tangency sits where present
consumption equals future consumption.

```python
a, b, px, py, m = 1, 1, 1, 1, 12
c1 = (a/(a+b)) * m/px
c2 = (b/(a+b)) * m/py
print("optimum c1 =", c1, " c2 =", c2)
print("on 45-degree line (c1 == c2)?", c1 == c2)
```

output:
```
optimum c1 = 6.0  c2 = 6.0
on 45-degree line (c1 == c2)? True
```

The optimum is (6, 6): equal present and future consumption, on the 45-degree
line, exactly the smoothing result the scene asserts. These numbers are our own.
`ok`.
