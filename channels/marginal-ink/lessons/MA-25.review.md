## Equation `gdpEq`  `Y = C + I + G + (X - M)`

> "GDP is C + I + G + (X – M)."
> — 19.1 Measuring the Size of the Economy: Gross Domestic Product
> (`213-19-1-measuring-the-size-of-the-economy-gross-domestic-product.md`)

Also from 24.2: "the formula for aggregate demand is AD = C + I + G + X - M".
Verbatim expenditure identity. **ok.**

## Equation `realEq`  `real Y = nominal Y / price level`

> "Nominal GDP (billions of dollars) / GDP Deflator (2012 = 100)"; the section
> explains converting nominal GDP to real GDP using a price index/deflator.
> — 19.2 Adjusting Nominal Values to Real Values
> (`214-19-2-adjusting-nominal-values-to-real-values.md`)

Real GDP = nominal GDP divided by the price level (deflator/100) is the standard
deflation relation stated in 19.2. **ok.**

## Equation `growthEq`  `Y = A · F(K, L)`

> "the components of economic growth, including physical capital, human capital,
> and technology"; "greater physical capital implies more output".
> — 20.3 Components of Economic Growth (`228-20-3-components-of-economic-growth.md`)

NOTE: standard macro construction. OpenStax 20.3 describes output as driven by
capital, labour and technology/productivity; the compact aggregate production
function Y = A·F(K,L) (output = productivity × function of capital and labour) is
the standard formal statement of exactly this, grounded in the cited chapter. **ok.**

## Equation `multiplierEq`  `k = 1 / (1 - MPC)`

> "A key concept in Keynesian economics is the expenditure multiplier."
> — 25.2 The Building Blocks of Keynesian Analysis (`292-25-2-...`);
> "The marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is the share of [an additional
> dollar consumed]".

NOTE: standard macro construction. OpenStax defines the expenditure multiplier
and the MPC, and the Keynesian-Cross appendix (`431-equilibrium-in-the-keynesian-cross-model.md`)
derives the geometric sum; the closed form of the simple spending multiplier is
k = 1/(1−MPC). (Distinct from the money multiplier 1/reserve ratio in Ch.27 —
this lesson correctly uses the MPC form.) Correct standard form. **ok.**

## Equation `taylorEq`  `i = r* + π + 0.5(π − π*) + 0.5 g`

> "monetary policy involves managing interest rates and credit conditions... The
> specific interest rate targeted in open market operations is the federal funds
> rate."
> — 28.3 How a Central Bank Executes Monetary Policy
> (`327-28-3-how-a-central-bank-executes-monetary-policy.md`)

NOTE: standard macro construction. OpenStax describes the central bank setting a
short-term policy rate in response to inflation and economic activity; the Taylor
rule i = r* + π + 0.5(π − π*) + 0.5g is the standard textbook construction of
that behaviour (Taylor 1993), grounded in the cited 28.3 chapter. The written
coefficients (0.5 on the inflation gap, 0.5 on the output gap) are the canonical
Taylor weights, and the narration correctly stresses the >1 response of the
nominal rate to inflation (the Taylor principle). Correct standard form. **ok.**

## Equation `debtEq`  `D_t = D_{t-1} + deficit_t`

## Equation `phillipsEq`  `π = π^e − β(u − u*)`

> "A Phillips curve illustrates a tradeoff between the unemployment rate and the
> inflation rate. If one is higher, the other must be lower."; "Phillips analyzed
> 60 years of British data and did find that tradeoff".
> — 25.3 The Phillips Curve (`293-25-3-the-phillips-curve.md`)

NOTE: standard macro construction. 25.3 states the short-run tradeoff and that
the curve shifts/breaks down once inflation is expected; the expectations-augmented
Phillips curve π = π^e − β(u − u*) is the standard formal form of exactly this,
and it reduces to the vertical long-run curve (verticalEq) when π = π^e ⇒ u = u*.
Correct standard form. **ok.**

## Equation `verticalEq`  `long run: u → u*`

> "In the neoclassical zone, the near-vertical SRAS curve close to the level of
> potential GDP"; the long-run Phillips curve / neoclassical result is that
> unemployment returns to its natural rate.
> — 25.3 (`293-25-3-...`), 26.1–26.2 Neoclassical perspective (`302-`, `303-`)

Consistent with the expectations-augmented Phillips curve above. **ok.**
