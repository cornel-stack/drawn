# Fact-check — MA-10

Lesson MA-10 derives the IS curve: the locus of interest-rate / output pairs at
which the goods market clears. It builds on the Keynesian cross (output equals
planned spending, Y = C + I + G), introduces the spending multiplier
1/(1 − MPC), and shows the curve slopes down because a lower interest rate lifts
investment and, through the multiplier, output. Two worked problems follow with
MPC = 0.8 (multiplier = 5) and ΔG = 20 (ΔY = 100).

Source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, CC BY 4.0 — Appendix D *The
Expenditure-Output Model* ("Equilibrium in the Keynesian Cross Model", file 431;
"The Multiplier Effect", file 432) and Ch 25 *The Keynesian Perspective* (§25.1
"Aggregate Demand in Keynesian Analysis", §25.2 "The Building Blocks of
Keynesian Analysis").

## IMPORTANT distinction — what is OpenStax and what is our construction

This follows the OpenStax-vs-standard split established in MA-05.

**FROM OPENSTAX Principles 3e** (cited with quotes + pages below):

- *Goods-market (expenditure-output) equilibrium: spending equals output,
  Y = C + I + G.* The Keynesian cross — aggregate expenditure crossing the
  45-degree line, 'where the total amount being spent on aggregate demand equals
  the total level of production' (file 431, p.887); 'Equilibrium occurs where
  AE = Y' (p.889). The four AD components C + I + G + net exports: §25.1
  (p.611). Grounds `eqEq`.
- *The marginal propensity to consume.* Disposable income as the determinant of
  consumption §25.1 (p.611); operationalised as 'the marginal propensity to
  consume is 0.9, since MPS + MPC = 1' (file 431, p.888). Grounds the MPC in
  `multEq`/`multEq2`/`given`/`given2`.
- *The multiplier = 1/(1 − MPC); a change in spending produces a larger change
  in output.* 'The MPC is equal to 1 – MPS... Therefore, the spending multiplier
  is:' with worked value 2.13 (file 432, p.893); 'a change in spending causes a
  more than proportionate change in GDP' (§25.2, p.617); the $100 → $213 worked
  multiplier (file 432, p.892-893). Grounds `multEq`, `multEq2`, and both
  problems.
- *Leakages (taxes, saving, imports) reduce the multiplier.* 'These three
  factors are known as "leakages," because they determine how much demand "leaks
  out" in each round of the multiplier effect... if the leakages are relatively
  large... the multiplier will be small' (file 432, p.893). Grounds the s3
  narration's leakage refinement.

**STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION — NOT in OpenStax Principles 3e.**
Flagged clearly, built by us FROM the Keynesian cross:

- the **IS curve** itself — the downward-sloping r–Y relationship and the "IS"
  label (`drawIS`, `markA`, `markB` in the s2 diagram);
- the **investment function** I = I0 − d·r (investment falling linearly with the
  interest rate);
- the chain **r↓ ⇒ I↑ ⇒ Y↑** traced across every interest rate into a curve
  (`isLogic`).

OpenStax Principles stops at the Keynesian cross (aggregate expenditure vs the
45-degree line) and never constructs the interest-rate–output locus, never
writes an investment function, and never names an IS curve. The economics IS in
the source — lower interest rates raise investment (§25.1, p.612; Table 25.1,
p.614) — but the IS curve that assembles that into a downward r–Y line is our
standard-macro layer. Nothing is copied.

## Equation `eqEq` — `Y = C + I + G`

> "The point where the aggregate expenditure line that is constructed from

Our `Y = C + I + G` is the closed-economy Keynesian cross (net exports dropped),

## Directional check — `isLogic` and the IS slope

```python
# I = I0 - d*r  (investment falls with the interest rate)
# Y = multiplier * (autonomous spending incl. I)
I0, d, mult = 100.0, 50.0, 5.0
A0 = 200.0   # other autonomous spending (illustrative)
def Y(r):
    I = I0 - d*r
    return mult*(A0 + I)
rs = [0.08, 0.04]   # high rate, then lower rate
for r in rs:
    print(f"r={r:.2f}  I={I0-d*r:.2f}  Y={Y(r):.2f}")
print("r down => I up:", (I0-d*rs[1]) > (I0-d*rs[0]))
print("r down => Y up:", Y(rs[1]) > Y(rs[0]))
```

output:
```
r=0.08  I=96.00  Y=1480.00
r=0.04  I=98.00  Y=1490.00
r down => I up: True
r down => Y up: True
```

Lowering the interest rate raises investment, which the multiplier turns into a
larger rise in output — so the r–Y locus slopes down, exactly as `isLogic` and
the s2 diagram (point B at the lower rate has higher output than point A) claim.
`ok`.
