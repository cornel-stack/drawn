# Fact-check — MA-05

Lesson MA-05 extends the Solow growth model (MA-04) with three additions:
population growth (which steepens the break-even line to (δ+n)k and lowers
steady-state capital per worker); technological progress (labour-augmenting
technology as the only source of sustained growth in output per head); and the
golden rule (the saving rate / capital level that maximises steady-state
consumption, where the slope of output equals the break-even slope). Two worked
problems follow on f(k)=√k with s=0.4, δ=0.1, n=0.1.

Qualitative source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, Ch 20 Economic
Growth, §20.3 "Components of Economic Growth" and §20.4 "Economic Convergence",
CC BY 4.0.

## IMPORTANT distinction — what is OpenStax and what is our construction

This follows the OpenStax-vs-standard-Solow split established in MA-04.

**Supported qualitatively by OpenStax Ch 20** (cited with quotes + pages below):

- *Technology / innovation as a component of growth and the source of sustained
  productivity gains.* §20.3 (p.489) + §20.4 (p.494): capital deepening alone
  runs into diminishing returns; only technology sidesteps them and sustains
  long-run growth in income per head. This grounds `techEq` qualitatively.
- *Population lowers income per head.* §20.3 (p.487): "Increases in population
  lower per capita income." This grounds the sign of the `breakEq`/`breakEq2`
  population-growth effect.
- *Capital deepening with DIMINISHING returns.* §20.3 (p.487) + §20.4
  (p.493-494). Grounds the concave f(k)=√k shape.
- *Convergence — poorer countries can grow faster and catch up.* §20.4
  (p.492-495) (context for Part 2, not a board item here).

**STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION — our Solow model, NOT in OpenStax
Principles.** Flagged clearly and layered by us on the OpenStax qualitative
content:

- the break-even line **(δ+n)k** and the steady-state condition
  **s·f(k*)=(δ+n)k*** (`breakEq`, `breakEq2`);
- labour-augmenting technology growth **g_y=g** (`techEq`);
- the golden rule **f'(k_gold)=δ+n** (`goldEq`);
- the per-worker production function **f(k)=√k** and all parameters
  (s=0.4, δ=0.1, n=0.1).

OpenStax Ch 20 never writes a production function in symbols, never defines a
saving, depreciation or population-growth rate, never writes a growth rate for
technology, and never defines a golden rule or marginal products. §20.4's Figure
20.7 is a *qualitative* capital-deepening/technology sketch with no equations.
The Solow formalism, its parameters and all numbers are our own standard-macro
construction. Nothing is copied.

## Equation `breakEq` — `s·f(k*) = (δ+n)k*`

growth). The economics it formalises is in the source — population lowers income

> "The result of having population in the denominator is mathematically

The break-even line (δ+n)k, the saving rate s, depreciation rate δ and

OUR construction; verified numerically under Problem s5. `ok`.

## Problem `s4` — steady-state capital with population growth (f(k)=√k, s=0.4, δ=0.1, n=0.1)

```python
s, delta, n = 0.4, 0.1, 0.1
sqrt_kstar = s/(delta+n)        # s√k = (δ+n)k  ⇒  √k* = s/(δ+n)
kstar = sqrt_kstar**2
print("sqrt(k*) = s/(delta+n) =", sqrt_kstar)
print("k* =", kstar)
print("compare n=0 case: k* = (s/delta)**2 =", (s/delta)**2)
print("consistency s*sqrt(k*) =", s*kstar**0.5, " (delta+n)*k* =", (delta+n)*kstar)
print("s4 check expr (0.4/(0.1+0.1))**2 =", (0.4/(0.1+0.1))**2)
```

output:
```

consistency s*sqrt(k*) = 0.8  (delta+n)*k* = 0.8
s4 check expr (0.4/(0.1+0.1))**2 = 4.0
```

**Verdict `ok`.**
- `step2`: √k* = s/(δ+n) = 0.4/0.2 = 2 — exact.
- `answer`: k* = 4 — exact.
- Narration's comparison: with n=0 the steady state was k*=(s/δ)²=16; adding
  n=0.1 quarters capital per worker (4 vs 16) — exact.
- Consistency: s·√k* = (δ+n)·k* = 0.8 at the steady state — investment equals
  break-even, as `breakEq` requires.
- `check.expr` `(0.4/(0.1+0.1))**2` = 4 (value 4, tol 1%) — matches.

## Diagram steady state (scene s2) — the plotted curves

The s2 diagram plots f(k) (outputCurve, blue), s·f(k) (savingCurve, green), the
old depreciation line δk with slope 0.3 (depLine, red, a=0.3), the new break-even
line (δ+n)k with slope 0.6 (breakLine, orange, a=0.6), and a `mark_point` at
(1, 0.6). The investment curve uses s·f = 0.6√k on that plot, so the diagram's
own parameters are s·f(k)=0.6√k against a break-even slope of 0.6:

```python
import sympy as sp
k = sp.symbols('k', positive=True)
sol = sp.solve(sp.Eq(0.6*sp.sqrt(k), 0.6*k), k)   # 0.6 sqrt(k) = 0.6 k
print("0.6 sqrt(k) = 0.6 k  -> k* =", sol)
print("investment at k*=1: 0.6*sqrt(1) =", 0.6*(1**0.5),
      " break-even 0.6*1 =", 0.6*1)
print("mark_point (1, 0.6) matches:", abs(0.6*(1**0.5)-0.6) < 1e-9)
```

output:
```
0.6 sqrt(k) = 0.6 k  -> k* = [1.00000000000000]
investment at k*=1: 0.6*sqrt(1) = 0.6  break-even 0.6*1 = 0.6
mark_point (1, 0.6) matches: True
```

The green investment curve (s·f=0.6√k) crosses the orange break-even line
((δ+n)k with slope 0.6) at k*=1 with investment = break-even = 0.6, matching the
`mark_point` at (1, 0.6). The steeper break-even line (0.6 vs the old 0.3)
pushes the crossing left to a lower steady state, as the narration describes.
Consistent. `ok`.
