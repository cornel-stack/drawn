# Fact-check — MA-08

Lesson MA-08 teaches unemployment: it builds the labour market supply-and-demand
diagram, shows how a wage held above equilibrium creates unemployment, defines
the unemployment rate, separates the natural rate (frictional + structural) from
cyclical unemployment (actual − natural) and full employment, then works two
problems on our own illustrative figures.

Source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, Ch 21 Unemployment — §21.1 "How
Economists Define and Compute Unemployment Rate", §21.3 "What Causes Changes in
Unemployment over the Short Run", §21.4 "What Causes Changes in Unemployment over
the Long Run", CC BY 4.0.

## This lesson is DIRECTLY from OpenStax (unlike the Solow lessons)

Every board relation in MA-08 is standard Principles content stated explicitly
in OpenStax Ch 21 — this is not a standard-intermediate-macro layer bolted onto
qualitative text (as MA-05/MA-06 were). The equations are compact restatements
of definitions the source gives in words and in a worked example:

- **Unemployment rate = unemployed / labour force**, labour force = employed +
  unemployed, with those not looking outside the labour force — §21.1, quoted
  below. The source even works the identical calculation (6.877m / 162.052m =
  4.2%).
- **The labour market diagram** — labour supply and demand, the equilibrium
  wage where the market clears, and a wage held above equilibrium (minimum wage,
  unions, efficiency wages, sticky/downwardly-rigid wages) producing a surplus
  of labour = unemployment — §21.3, Figures 21.5 and 21.6, quoted below.
- **Natural rate = frictional + structural** — §21.4, quoted below.
- **Cyclical unemployment from recessions = actual − natural**, and **full
  employment = only the natural rate remains** — §21.3/§21.4, quoted below.

Only the two problems' input numbers are ours (changed from the source's worked
example, not copied). Nothing is flagged as out-of-source: all four board
relations are standard, textbook-stated Principles material.

## Equation `rateEq` — `u = U / (E + U)`

DIRECTLY FROM OpenStax §21.1.

> without jobs, but rather the percentage of adults who are in the labor force

> "Labor force: the number of employed plus the unemployed." (§21.1, p.503;

## Equation `naturalEq` — `u_nat = u_frictional + u_structural`

DIRECTLY FROM OpenStax §21.4.

> "Economists have a term to describe the remaining level of unemployment that
> occurs even when the economy is healthy: they call it the natural rate of
> unemployment." (§21.4, p.515; source lines 29-30)

Its two components each get a named subsection:

> "Economists call the unemployment that occurs in the meantime, as workers move
> between jobs, frictional unemployment." (§21.4, p.515-516; source lines 49-50)

> "Another factor that influences the natural rate of unemployment is the amount
> of structural unemployment. The structurally unemployed are individuals who
> have no jobs because they lack skills valued by the labor market, either
> because demand has shifted away from the skills they do have, or because they
> never learned any skills." (§21.4, p.516; source lines 84-87)

The narration that frictional is healthy — §21.4 "Frictional unemployment is not
inherently a bad thing" (p.516, source lines 50-51). `ok`.

## Equation `cyclicalEq` — `u_cyclical = u_actual − u_nat`

DIRECTLY FROM OpenStax §21.3/§21.4.

> "Economists call the variation in unemployment that the economy causes moving
> from expansion to recession or from recession to expansion (i.e. the business
> cycle) cyclical unemployment." (§21.3, p.511; source lines 38-40)

> "The rise in unemployment that occurs because of a recession is cyclical
> unemployment." (§21.4, p.515; source lines 7-8)

That the actual rate is natural + cyclical, so cyclical is the gap above the
natural rate, follows directly from the full-employment definition:

> "Economists consider the economy to be at full employment when the actual
> unemployment rate is equal to the natural unemployment rate." (§21.4, p.516;
> source lines 99-100)

i.e. cyclical unemployment is exactly actual − natural, zero at full employment.
The s2/s3 sticky-wage mechanism (wage held above equilibrium → quantity supplied
exceeds quantity demanded → unemployment) is §21.3 "Why Wages Might Be Sticky
Downward" and Figure 21.6:

> "Because the wage rate is stuck at W, above the equilibrium, the number of
> those who want jobs (Qs) is greater than the number of job openings (Qd). The
> result is unemployment." (§21.3 Figure 21.6, p.513; source lines 124-126)

The causes the narration lists — minimum wage, union contracts, efficiency
wages — are §21.3 (p.512): minimum wage ("it is illegal to reduce their wages"),
union contracts ("wage cuts might violate the contract"), and "Efficiency wage
theory argues that workers' productivity depends on their pay, and so employers
will often find it worthwhile to pay their employees somewhat more than market
conditions might dictate" (source lines 68-70, 91-92). `ok`.
