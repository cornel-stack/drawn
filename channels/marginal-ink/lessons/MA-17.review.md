## Equation `apprEq`  — `E↑ = appreciation`

> "When the exchange rate for a currency rises, so that the currency exchanges for more
> of other currencies, we refer to it as appreciating or 'strengthening.' When the exchange rate for a currency
> falls, so that a currency trades for less of other currencies, we refer to it as depreciating or 'weakening.'"
> — §29.1 *How the Foreign Exchange Market Works*, "Strengthening and Weakening Currency", p.700

Supporting (exchange rate is a price):

> "In reality, the exchange rate is a price—the price of one
> currency expressed in terms of units of another currency."
> — §29.1, p.696

> "An exchange rate is nothing more than a price—that is, the price of one
> currency in terms of another currency—and so we can analyze it with the tools of supply and demand."
> — §29.1 intro, p.696

The source **literally** calls the exchange rate "a price". Confirmed.

## Equation `bopEq`  — `CA + FA = 0`

**NOTE:** This is the standard balance-of-payments identity. OpenStax states it
in words, not as the algebraic equation `CA+FA=0`. The algebraic form is the
standard national-accounts construction, and the lesson header (lines 2-4 of the
YAML) explicitly flags it as such. Closest supporting prose:

> "To economists, a trade surplus literally means the same thing as an outflow of
> financial capital, and a trade deficit literally means the same thing as an inflow of financial capital."
> — §23.3 *Trade Balances and Flows of Financial Capital*, p.565

> "The connection between trade balances and international flows of financial capital is so close that economists
> sometimes describe the balance of trade as the balance of payments. Each category of the current account
> balance involves a corresponding flow of payments between a given country and the rest of the world economy."
> — §23.3, "The Balance of Trade as the Balance of Payments", p.566

> "Flow of goods and services (lines one and three) show up in the current
> account, while we find flow of funds (lines two and four) in the financial account."
> — §23.3, p.566

Intro-chapter framing that a trade deficit is financed by capital inflow:

> "At some point, foreign investors become pessimistic about the economy
> and move their money to other countries." … "the intimate connection between international
> flows of goods and services and international flows of financial capital, which to economists are really just two
> sides of the same coin."
> — Introduction to International Trade and Capital Flows, p.558

Verdict `ok` with the NOTE above: the words match; the algebraic CA+FA=0 is the
standard construction and is flagged in the lesson.

## Problem `s4`

```python
# s4: percentage appreciation of the pound
E0, E1 = 1.25, 1.50
appr = (E1 - E0) / E0
print("appreciation =", appr, "=", appr * 100, "%")
# check block: expr=(1.50-1.25)/1.25, value=0.20, tol=0.01
print("check:", (1.50 - 1.25) / 1.25, "vs 0.20  match:", abs((1.50 - 1.25) / 1.25 - 0.20) <= 0.01)
```

output:
```
appreciation = 0.2 = 20.0 %
check: 0.2 vs 0.20  match: True
```

Boxed answer `0.20 = 20%` confirmed. Check block agrees within tol.
