# Fact-check — MA-09

Lesson MA-09 teaches how banks create money from the balance sheet: fractional
reserve banking (a bank keeps the reserve fraction of each deposit and lends the
rest), the round-by-round money-creation chain (loans become deposits that are
lent again), the money multiplier = 1/(reserve ratio), the total money created =
deposit / reserve ratio, the geometric-series derivation behind the multiplier,
the standard caveats (excess reserves and cash holdings shrink the real
multiplier; money is destroyed in reverse), then works two problems on our own
illustrative figures.

Source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, Ch 27 Money and Banking — §27.3
"The Role of Banks" and §27.4 "How Banks Create Money", CC BY 4.0.

## This lesson is DIRECTLY from OpenStax

Every board relation is standard Principles content stated explicitly in Ch 27.
The one exception is `totalEq`, the geometric-series form, which is the standard
algebra behind the multiplier that OpenStax states as a closed-form formula
rather than printing the series. Only the two problems' input numbers are ours
(the £1000 deposit; the 0.1 reserve ratio is the source's own value, kept as a
clean illustrative figure). Note that OpenStax applies the multiplier to the
*excess reserves* ($9m → $90m), whereas the lesson applies the same formula to
the *full deposit* (£1000 → £10,000); both are the identical multiplier and the
standard way the result is presented.

## Equation `multEq2` — `total = deposit / rr` (boxed)

DIRECTLY FROM OpenStax §27.4 — the money multiplier applied to the inflow.

> "We then multiply the money multiplier by the change in excess reserves to
> determine the total amount of M1 money supply created in the banking system."
> (§27.4, p.660; source lines 79-80)

$9m × (1/0.10) = $90m. The lesson applies the same multiplier to the full
deposit: total = deposit × (1/rr) = deposit/rr. `ok`.

## Equation `totalEq` — `1 + (1−rr) + (1−rr)² + ⋯ = 1/rr`

STANDARD ALGEBRA behind the multiplier. OpenStax traces the chain (Singleton
lends $9m → redeposited at First National, which lends $8.1m → redeposited at
Second National, …) and gives the closed form, but does not print the series:

> "This tells us by how many times a loan will be 'multiplied' as it is spent in
> the economy and then re-deposited in other banks." (§27.4, p.660; source lines
> 72-73)

> "Now, First National must hold only 10% as required reserves ($900,000) but can
> lend out the other 90% ($8.1 million)." (§27.4, p.659-660; source lines 56-57)

Each round's lending shrinks by the factor (1−rr), so the deposits created sum to
the geometric series 1 + (1−rr) + (1−rr)² + ⋯ = 1/rr — the textbook derivation of
the multiplier. Verified numerically below (sum = 10.0 = 1/0.1). `ok`.

## Caveats in the s3 narration

Both cautions are DIRECTLY from §27.4:

> "Additionally, a bank can also choose to hold extra reserves ... When an economy
> is in recession, banks are likely to hold a higher proportion of reserves."
> (§27.4 Cautions, p.661; source lines 92-94)

> "the money multiplier depends on people re-depositing the money that they
> receive in the banking system. If people instead store their cash ... banks
> cannot recirculate the money in the form of loans." (§27.4, p.661; source lines
> 101-103)

The "runs in reverse / money destroyed" point is the standard corollary of the
same chain (loans repaid or deposits withdrawn contract the multiplier), implicit
in §27.4's statement that all money except the original reserves is a result of
re-deposited and re-lent loans (p.661, source lines 99-100). Consistent with
source. `ok`.

## Problem `s4` — the money multiplier (rr = 0.1)

```python
rr = 0.1
mult = 1 / rr
print("money multiplier 1/rr = 1/%s = %s" % (rr, mult))
print("s4 check expr '1 / 0.1' =", 1 / 0.1, "(boxed value 10)")
# geometric series behind the multiplier
s = sum((1 - rr) ** n for n in range(2000))
print("series 1+(1-rr)+(1-rr)^2+... =", round(s, 6), "-> 1/rr =", 1 / rr)
```

output:
```
money multiplier 1/rr = 1/0.1 = 10.0
s4 check expr '1 / 0.1' = 10.0 (boxed value 10)
series 1+(1-rr)+(1-rr)^2+... = 10.0 -> 1/rr = 10.0
```

**Verdict `ok`.**
- `step1`/`answer`: multiplier = 1/0.1 = 10 — exact, matches boxed value 10.
- `totalEq`: the geometric series sums to 10.0 = 1/rr — confirms the derivation.
- `check.expr` `1 / 0.1` = 10.0 (value 10, tol 1%) — matches.
