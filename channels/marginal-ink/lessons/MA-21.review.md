# Fact-check — MA-21

## FLAGGED SOURCE — honest treatment

Intro OpenStax (Principles of Economics 3e) does **not** formally present real
business cycle (RBC) theory. This lesson is grounded in the adjacent OpenStax
**neoclassical perspective** (§26) and **economic-growth/productivity** (§20)
content, and the lesson header (lines 2–5 of the YAML) states this plainly.

What OpenStax **does** supply, in words, and is quoted below:

- **Output = productivity × function of inputs** — the aggregate production
  function (§20.2) and that growth/output comes from worker productivity (§20.2).
  This grounds `prodEq` and `shockEq`.
- **The economy fluctuates around potential GDP with flexible prices/wages**, and
  potential GDP is set by the supply side (productivity, technology, resources) —
  §26.1. This grounds the neoclassical frame and `given2`.
- **Neoclassical skepticism of active stabilisation** — active policy "is likely
  to exacerbate the cycles rather than dampen them" (§26.2). This grounds `policyEq`.

What is the **standard RBC (Kydland–Prescott) construction**, NOT formally in
intro OpenStax (marked `ok` with a NOTE on the relevant equations):

- That **productivity (technology) shocks are *the* cause of the business cycle**.
- **Intertemporal substitution of labour**, `labourEq` `w = A·MPL` — workers
  rationally reschedule work toward high-wage (high-productivity) periods.
- That **recessions are the efficient / optimal response** to a real shock.

These are the standard construction built on OpenStax's neoclassical building
blocks (which are quoted) and are flagged in the lesson header. No economic claim
in the lesson contradicts the source — the gap is a *formalisation* gap, not a
factual error.

## Equation `prodEq`  —  `Y = A · F(K, L)`

> "To analyze the sources of economic growth, it is useful to think about a
> **production function, which is the technical relationship by which economic
> inputs like labor, machinery, and raw materials are turned into outputs** like
> goods and services that consumers use."
> — §20.2 Labor Productivity and Economic Growth, p.481

> "Sustained long-term economic growth comes from increases in worker
> productivity … **Labor productivity is the value that each employed person
> creates per unit of their input.**"
> — §20.2, p.480

OpenStax states output is a function of inputs (capital/labour) and that
productivity scales what those inputs produce. Writing this as `Y = A·F(K,L)`
with `A` = total factor productivity is the standard formalisation of the
aggregate production function OpenStax describes in words. **Verdict: ok.**

## Equation `labourEq`  —  `w = A·MPL`

> "The neoclassical perspective … holds that, in the long run, the economy will
> fluctuate around its potential GDP … wages and prices will adjust in a flexible
> manner." — §26.1, p.645

**NOTE:** standard real-business-cycle (Kydland–Prescott) construction. Intro
OpenStax covers the neoclassical building blocks in words (flexible wages, output
set by productivity — quoted above), but the specific claim that the wage equals
productivity times the marginal product of labour, driving *intertemporal
substitution of labour*, is the formal RBC construction and is not presented in
intro OpenStax. Flagged in the lesson header. The claim is economically standard
and consistent with the OpenStax neoclassical frame. **Verdict: ok.**
