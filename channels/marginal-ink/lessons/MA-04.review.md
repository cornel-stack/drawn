# Fact-check — MA-04

Lesson MA-04 builds the Solow growth model diagram and its steady state: output
per worker as a concave function of capital per worker (diminishing returns to
capital); investment per worker as a scaled copy s·f(k); depreciation per worker
as the straight line δk; the steady state k* where investment just replaces
worn-out capital so capital per worker stops changing; the stability of that
crossing; the result that a higher saving rate raises the *level* of capital and
output per worker but not the long-run *growth rate*; and a convergence
prediction (poor countries far below their steady state grow faster and catch
up). Two worked problems follow on f(k)=√k, s=0.4, δ=0.1.

Qualitative source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, Ch 20 Economic
Growth, §20.2 "Labor Productivity and Economic Growth", §20.3 "Components of
Economic Growth", §20.4 "Economic Convergence", CC BY 4.0.

## IMPORTANT distinction — what is OpenStax and what is our construction

**Supported qualitatively by OpenStax Ch 20** (cited with quotes + pages below):

- *Labour productivity = output per worker.* §20.2 (p.481).
- *Capital deepening — more capital per worker raises output per worker, with
  DIMINISHING returns.* §20.3 (capital deepening, p.487) + §20.4 (the explicit
  diminishing-returns-to-capital statement, p.493-494).
- *The components of growth — physical capital, human capital, technology.*
  §20.3 (p.487).
- *Convergence — poorer countries can grow faster and catch up.* §20.4
  (p.492-493), which supports the s3 convergence narration.

**STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION — our Solow model, NOT in OpenStax
Principles.** The formal Solow steady-state diagram and all its equations are
layered by us on top of the OpenStax qualitative content:

- the steady-state condition `ssEq` s·f(k*)=δk*;
- the capital-accumulation law of motion `accEq` Δk = s·f(k) − δk and its
  steady state `ssEq2`;
- the per-worker production function **f(k)=√k**;
- the saving rate **s** and depreciation rate **δ** (and the diagram's own
  f(k)=2√k, s·f=0.6√k, δ=0.3).

OpenStax Ch 20 never writes a production function in symbols, never defines a
saving rate or a depreciation rate, and never draws the investment-equals-
depreciation diagram; §20.4's Figure 20.7 is a *qualitative* capital-deepening/
technology sketch with no equations. The Solow formalism, its parameters and all
numbers are our own standard-macro construction. Nothing is copied.

## Equation `ssEq` — `s·f(k*) = δk*`

STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION (our Solow model). The economics it
formalises is in the source — capital deepening and diminishing returns:

> "When society increases the level of capital per person, we call the result
> capital deepening. The idea of capital deepening can apply both to additional
> human capital per worker and to additional physical capital per worker."
> (§20.3, p.487; source lines 66-68)

> "Again, greater physical capital implies more output." (§20.3, p.487; source
> line 36)

> "A first argument is based on diminishing marginal returns. Even though
> deepening human and physical capital will tend to increase GDP per capita, the
> law of diminishing returns suggests that as an economy continues to increase
> its human and physical capital, the marginal gains to economic growth will
> diminish." (§20.4, p.493; source lines 117-120)

The saving rate s, depreciation rate δ and the balance condition
investment=depreciation are our Solow parameterisation. `ok`.

## Equation `accEq` — `Δk = s·f(k) − δk`

STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION (Solow law of motion; not in OpenStax).
Its two forces are each in the source — investment building future capital, and
the concave (diminishing-returns) shape of f(k):

> "Human capital and physical capital accumulation are similar: In both cases,
> investment now pays off in higher productivity in the future." (§20.3, p.487;
> source lines 40-41)

> "the way in which the line starts out steeper on the left but then flattens as
> it moves to the right shows the diminishing marginal returns, as additional
> marginal amounts of capital deepening increase output by ever-smaller
> amounts." (§20.4, p.494; source lines 158-161)

Depreciation δk and the exact difference equation are ours. `ok`.

## Equation `ssEq2` — `Δk = 0 ⇒ s·f(k*) = δk*`

STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION (steady state of `accEq`; not in
OpenStax). The "cross exactly once, stable" claim follows from the concavity of
f(k) that §20.4 (p.494, quoted under `accEq`) states qualitatively. The s3
narration's convergence prediction attached to this equation — poor countries
below their steady state grow faster and catch up — IS supported by §20.4; see
the convergence quotes below. `ok`.

## Convergence narration (scene s3) — supported by §20.4

The s3 claim that a country far below its steady state grows fast while a rich
country near its steady state grows slowly, so the poor country catches up
("convergence"), is directly supported:

> "Some low-income and middle-income economies around the world have shown a
> pattern of convergence, in which their economies grow faster than those of
> high-income countries." (§20.4, p.492; source lines 9-11)

> "Low-income countries like China and India tend to have lower levels of human
> capital and physical capital, so an investment in capital deepening should
> have a larger marginal effect in these countries than in high-income
> countries, where levels of human and physical capital are already relatively
> high. Diminishing returns implies that low-income economies could converge to
> the levels that the high-income countries achieve." (§20.4, p.493; source
> lines 131-134)

The s3 caveat that convergence "does not always happen, because saving rates,
population growth and technology differ" matches §20.4's own hedging:

> "Will this pattern of economic convergence persist into the future? This is a
> controversial question among economists" (§20.4, p.493; source lines 112-113);
> "Most healthy, growing economies are deepening their human and physical
> capital and increasing technology at the same time. … The gains from
> technology can offset the diminishing returns involved with capital deepening."
> (§20.4, p.495; source lines 182-187)

The components of growth the narration holds fixed (technology) and names are
§20.3's own list:

> "the components of economic growth, including physical capital, human capital,
> and technology" (§20.3, p.487; source lines 28 & 33-34); "technology is
> typically the most important contributor to U.S. economic growth" (§20.3,
> p.489; source lines 121-122).

`ok`.

## Problem `s4` — steady-state capital (f(k)=√k, s=0.4, δ=0.1)

```python
s, delta = 0.4, 0.1
sqrt_kstar = s/delta          # s√k = δk  ⇒  √k* = s/δ
kstar = sqrt_kstar**2
print("sqrt(k*) = s/delta =", sqrt_kstar)
print("k* =", kstar)
print("output f(k*) = sqrt(k*) =", kstar**0.5)
print("consistency  s*sqrt(k*) =", s*kstar**0.5, " delta*k* =", delta*kstar)
print("s4 check expr (0.4/0.1)**2 =", (0.4/0.1)**2)
```

output:
```

k* = 16.0
output f(k*) = sqrt(k*) = 4.0
consistency  s*sqrt(k*) = 1.6  delta*k* = 1.6
s4 check expr (0.4/0.1)**2 = 16.0
```

**Verdict `ok`.**
- `step2`: √k* = s/δ = 0.4/0.1 = 4 — exact.
- `answer`: k* = 16 — exact; output f(k*)=√16=4 as the narration states.
- Consistency: s·√k* = δ·k* = 1.6 at the steady state — investment equals
  depreciation, as `ssEq` requires.
- `check.expr` `(0.4/0.1)**2` = 16 (value 16, tol 1%) — matches.

## Problem `s5` — steady-state consumption (same economy)

```python

kstar = (s/delta)**2          # 16
fkstar = kstar**0.5           # 4
cstar  = (1-s)*fkstar
invest = s*fkstar
print("f(k*) =", fkstar)
print("c* = (1-s)f(k*) =", cstar)
print("investment s*f(k*) =", invest, " delta*k* =", delta*kstar,

```

```
c* = (1-s)f(k*) = 2.4

s5 check expr (1-0.4)*4 = 2.4
```

- Consistency: investment = 0.4·4 = 1.6 = δk* = 0.1·16 = 1.6 — the un-consumed

- `check.expr` `(1-0.4)*4` = 2.4 (value 2.4, tol 1%) — matches.
