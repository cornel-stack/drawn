# Fact-check — MA-16

All evidence below quotes OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, §30.3 *Federal
Deficits and the National Debt* (CC BY 4.0), source file
`principles-economics-3e/352-30-3-federal-deficits-and-the-national-debt.md`,
PDF pp.730-731 (manifest pages 746-748).

**Honest scope note.** The *qualitative* content of this lesson — deficit vs
debt, debt = accumulated deficits minus surpluses, the debt/GDP ratio as the
meaningful measure, and the growth-vs-debt mechanism whereby the ratio can fall
even while deficits are run — is explicitly in the OpenStax source and is quoted
below. The *formal* algebra — the one-period budget-constraint identity
`D_t = D_{t-1} + deficit` with `deficit = (G−T) + rD`, and the debt-ratio
dynamics `Δb ≈ (G−T)/Y + (r−g)b` — is the **standard government-budget-constraint
construction**, which this intro OpenStax section states only in words. The
lesson header (lines 3-4 of `MA-16.yaml`) already flags this. The ids `debtEq`,
`constraintEq` and `ratioDyn` (the latter not in the review frontmatter but
present on the board of s3) are therefore verified against the closest
supporting OpenStax prose plus the standard identity, and the distinction is
noted on each entry below.

## Equation `ratioEq`

`b = D / Y` — the debt-to-GDP ratio is the meaningful measure.

> "Another useful way to view the budget deficit is through the prism of
> accumulated debt rather than annual deficits. … Figure 30.8 shows the ratio of
> debt/GDP since 1966."

— OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, §30.3, p.730-731. The whole "Debt/GDP
Ratio" subsection treats debt relative to GDP as the number economists watch,
supporting the lesson's claim that the ratio, not the raw dollar figure, is what
matters. Verdict: **ok**.

## Equation `defEq`

`deficit = G − T`.

> "the annual budget deficit or surplus, which is the difference between the tax
> revenue collected and spending over a fiscal year"

— OpenStax §30.3, p.730. Deficit = spending − tax revenue, exactly `G − T`.
Verdict: **ok**.

## Equation `debtEq`

`D_t = D_{t-1} + deficit_t` (boxed). Standard stock-flow recurrence.

> "The national debt refers to the total amount that the government has borrowed
> over time. In contrast, the budget deficit refers to how much the government
> has borrowed in one particular year."

> "Federal debt is the sum of annual budget deficits and surpluses."

— OpenStax §30.3, p.730-731. The source states in words that the debt is the
accumulated total of past annual deficits (minus surpluses); running that
accumulation one year forward gives exactly `D_t = D_{t-1} + deficit_t`. NOTE:
this is the standard government-budget-constraint identity; the OpenStax intro
source states it only in words (quoted above), and the algebraic recurrence is
the standard construction, flagged in the lesson header. Verdict: **ok**.

## Equation `constraintEq`

`deficit = (G − T)_primary + rD` — splitting the deficit into the primary
deficit plus interest on existing debt.

> "interest payments by the federal government also fell by about 1.0% of GDP"

— OpenStax §30.3, p.731. The source treats interest payments as a component of
federal spending (hence of the deficit), consistent with isolating `rD` as the
interest term. NOTE: the explicit primary-vs-interest decomposition is the
standard government-budget-constraint identity, not written as an equation in
this intro OpenStax section; the algebraic form is the standard construction,
flagged in the lesson header. Verdict: **ok**.

## Problem `s4`


output: `1.0`  (matches boxed `ΔD = 1.0 $tn`). Half the increase (0.5) is

value 1.0, tol 1% — agrees. Verdict: **ok**.

## Problem `s5`
