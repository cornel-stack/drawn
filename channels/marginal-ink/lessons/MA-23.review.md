# Fact-check — MA-23

**SOURCE FLAG (honest note, per lesson header).** Intro OpenStax *Principles of
Economics 3e* does **not** formally present the QE/ZLB mechanics as symbolic
equations. What it *does* cover in §28.3 "How a Central Bank Executes Monetary
Policy" (pp.673–678) and §22.4 "The Confusion Over Inflation" (p.631):

- **Open market operations** — the central bank buys/sells U.S. Treasury bonds
  to change bank reserves and the level of interest rates; buying bonds lowers
  interest rates and expands the money supply.
- **Quantitative easing is NAMED and described** — "an innovative and
  nontraditional policy known as quantitative easing (QE). This is the purchase
  of long-term government and private mortgage-backed securities by central
  banks to make credit available so as to stimulate aggregate demand"; it worked
  by "pushing long term interest rates down".
- **Zero lower bound in words** — in late 2008 the Fed "had already reduced the
  interest rate to near-zero ... it was impossible to stimulate the economy any
  further by lowering short term rates because they were already as low as they
  could get."
- **Real rate = nominal − inflation** — §22.4 states the Fisher relation
  arithmetically (4% nominal − 5% inflation ⇒ −1% real).

The lesson's **symbolic** QE/ZLB mechanics — `zlbEq` (i ≥ 0), `convEq`
(i↓⇒spending↑), `buyEq` (new reserves → buy long bonds), `priceEq`
(price↑⇒yield↓), `portEq` (yield = coupon/price) and `debateEq` (long rates↓,
asset prices↑) — are the **standard QE/zero-lower-bound construction**. Intro
OpenStax covers open market operations and names QE in words (quotes below); the
formal ZLB/QE identities are the standard construction, flagged in the lesson
header. No economic/financial claim in the lesson contradicts the source: the
bond price–yield relationship is correct (price up ⇒ yield down), and a negative
real rate at a zero nominal rate with positive inflation is correct.

## Equation `realEq`

`r = i - \pi^e` — the real interest rate is the nominal rate minus expected
inflation. Standard Fisher identity; OpenStax §22.4 states it arithmetically:

> "if a person has money in a bank account that pays 4% interest, but inflation
> rises to 5%, then the real rate of return for the money invested in that bank
> account is negative 1%." — *Principles 3e* §22.4 The Confusion Over Inflation,
> p.631 (text p.62)

> "If inflation is 0%, then the real interest rate is 5% ... However, if
> inflation is 5%, then the real interest rate is zero ... If inflation is 10%,
> then the real interest rate is negative 5%." — §22.4, p.631

NOTE: OpenStax states real = nominal − inflation in words/arithmetic, not as the
symbolic identity `r = i − π^e`. The symbolic form is the standard Fisher
identity.

## Equation `portEq`

`yield = coupon/price` — a bond's yield is its payment divided by its price.
This is the standard perpetuity / fixed-coupon identity behind QE's
price↑⇒yield↓ effect. OpenStax describes the effect, not the identity:

> "By offering to purchase mortgage-backed securities, the Fed was both pushing
> long term interest rates down and also removing possibly 'toxic assets' from
> the balance sheets of private financial firms." — §28.3, p.678

> "Therefore, Chairman Bernanke sought to lower long-term rates utilizing
> quantitative easing." — §28.3, p.678

NOTE: standard QE / bond price–yield construction; intro OpenStax covers open
market operations and names QE in words (quoted), the formal yield = coupon/price
mechanics are the standard construction, flagged in the lesson header.
