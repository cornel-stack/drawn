# Fact-check — MA-06

Lesson MA-06 teaches growth accounting: it decomposes an economy's output
growth into contributions from physical capital, labour, and technology, builds
the stacked bar, derives the Solow residual (ΔA/A) as what is left after capital
and labour are accounted for, and argues that this residual — total factor
productivity — is usually the largest single source of growth. Two worked
problems follow on our own illustrative figures (ΔY/Y=4%, ΔK/K=6%, ΔL/L=1%,
α=0.3).

Qualitative source: OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e*, Ch 20 Economic
Growth, §20.2 "Labor Productivity and Economic Growth" and §20.3 "Components of
Economic Growth", CC BY 4.0.

## IMPORTANT distinction — what is OpenStax and what is our construction

This follows the OpenStax-vs-standard-Solow split established in MA-05.

**Supported qualitatively by OpenStax Ch 20** (cited with quotes + pages below):

- *The three components / sources of growth — physical capital, human
  capital/labour, and technology.* §20.3 (p.487): "In this module, we discuss
  some of the components of economic growth, including physical capital, human
  capital, and technology." The per-capita production function's inputs are
  "the average level of human capital per person, the average level of physical
  capital per person, and the level of technology per person" (§20.3, p.487).
  §20.3's third lesson: these "three factors of human capital, physical capital,
  and technology work together" (p.490). This grounds the bar's three pieces and
  the gaEq/gaEq2 three-term decomposition.
- *Technology / innovation as a — usually the — key driver of growth.* §20.3
  (p.489): "technology is typically the most important contributor to U.S.
  economic growth. Growth in human capital and physical capital often explains
  only half or less than half of the economic growth that occurs. New ways of
  doing things are tremendously important." Technology is the "joker in the
  deck" (§20.3, p.487). This grounds the lesson's central claim that the green
  residual is usually the tallest piece.
- *The growth-accounting method and the residual attributed to technology.*
  §20.3 Growth Accounting Studies (p.489): "The usual approach uses an aggregate
  production function to estimate how much of per capita economic growth can be
  attributed to growth in physical capital and human capital. We can measure
  these two inputs at least roughly. The part of growth that is unexplained by
  measured inputs, called the residual, is then attributed to growth in
  technology." This grounds residEq *as a method* (capital and labour measured,
  technology inferred by subtraction).
- *Labour productivity = output per worker.* §20.2 (p.481): "Labor productivity
  is the value that each employed person creates per unit of their input." Its
  determinants are human capital, technological change and economies of scale
  (§20.2, p.481-482). Grounds the labour/human-capital component.

**STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION — the growth-accounting algebra, NOT
in OpenStax Principles.** Flagged clearly and layered by us on the OpenStax
qualitative components above:

- the **growth-accounting equation** ΔY/Y = α·ΔK/K + (1−α)·ΔL/L + ΔA/A
  (`gaEq`, `gaEq2`);
- the **Solow residual / total factor productivity** ΔA/A computed by
  subtraction, ΔA/A = ΔY/Y − α·ΔK/K − (1−α)·ΔL/L (`residEq`);
- the use of **income shares α and (1−α)** as the weights on capital and labour
  growth;
- the phrase **"measure of our ignorance"** for the residual;
- all numbers (ΔY/Y=4%, ΔK/K=6%, ΔL/L=1%, α=0.3) and both problems.

OpenStax Ch 20 describes growth accounting only in words: it never writes the
decomposition equation, never introduces income-share weights α/(1−α), never
writes a ΔA/A residual term symbolically, and never uses "total factor
productivity" or "measure of our ignorance". §20.3 states the residual *idea*
(unexplained growth attributed to technology) but gives no formula. The
equation, the weighting, the residual-by-subtraction computation and all numbers
are our own standard-macro construction. Nothing is copied.

income-share weights). The three sources it decomposes are the source's
components of growth:

> "In this module, we discuss some of the components of economic growth,
> including physical capital, human capital, and technology." (§20.3, p.487;
> source line 33-34)

> "Again, greater physical capital implies more output." (§20.3, p.487; source
> line 36)

The method itself:

> "The usual approach uses an aggregate production function to estimate how much
> of per capita economic growth can be attributed to growth in physical capital
> and human capital... The part of growth that is unexplained by measured
> inputs, called the residual, is then attributed to growth in technology."
> (§20.3, p.489; source lines 114-117)

The symbolic equation, the α/(1−α) income-share weights, and the explicit ΔA/A
term are OUR construction. `ok`.

## Equation `gaEq2` — `ΔY/Y = α·ΔK/K + (1−α)·ΔL/L + ΔA/A`

STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION (identical identity restated in the
derivation; not in OpenStax). The s3 narration — the weights "are shares of

> "Growth in technology is impossible to measure with a simple line on a graph."
> (§20.3, p.488; source line 94-95)

OUR construction. `ok`.

## Equation `residEq` — `ΔA/A = ΔY/Y − α·ΔK/K − (1−α)·ΔL/L`

STANDARD INTERMEDIATE-MACRO CONSTRUCTION (the Solow residual / total factor
productivity, computed by subtraction; not written symbolically in OpenStax).
The method is exactly the source's qualitative description:

> "The part of growth that is unexplained by measured inputs, called the
> residual, is then attributed to growth in technology." (§20.3, p.489; source
> lines 116-117)

The lesson's claim that the residual is usually the largest source of growth:

> "First, technology is typically the most important contributor to U.S.
> economic growth. Growth in human capital and physical capital often explains
> only half or less than half of the economic growth that occurs." (§20.3,
> p.489; source lines 121-123)

The s3 narration's cross-country, cross-time and cross-industry applications and
the "measure of our ignorance" label are standard growth-accounting commentary,
not source text. The term "total factor productivity", the "measure of our
ignorance" remark, and the subtraction formula are OUR construction. `ok`.

## Problem `s4` — the Solow residual (ΔY/Y=4%, ΔK/K=6%, ΔL/L=1%, α=0.3)

```python
dY, dK, dL, alpha = 0.04, 0.06, 0.01, 0.3
cap   = alpha*dK          # capital contribution  α·ΔK/K
lab   = (1-alpha)*dL      # labour contribution  (1−α)·ΔL/L
resid = dY - cap - lab    # residual  ΔA/A = ΔY/Y − cap − lab
print("capital contribution  alpha*dK/K =", cap, "=", round(cap*100,1), "%")
print("labour contribution (1-a)*dL/L  =", lab, "=", round(lab*100,1), "%")
print("residual dA/A                    =", resid, "=", round(resid*100,1), "%")
print("s4 check expr 0.04 - 0.3*0.06 - 0.7*0.01 =", 0.04 - 0.3*0.06 - 0.7*0.01)
```

output:
```
capital contribution  alpha*dK/K = 0.018 = 1.8 %
labour contribution (1-a)*dL/L  = 0.006999999999999999 = 0.7 %
residual dA/A                    = 0.015000000000000003 = 1.5 %
s4 check expr 0.04 - 0.3*0.06 - 0.7*0.01 = 0.015000000000000003
```

**Verdict `ok`.**
- `step1`/`step2`: capital contributes α·ΔK/K = 0.3·6 = 1.8%, labour (1−α)·ΔL/L
  = 0.7·1 = 0.7% — exact.
- `answer`: ΔA/A = 4 − 1.8 − 0.7 = 1.5% — exact.
- `check.expr` `0.04 - 0.3*0.06 - 0.7*0.01` = 0.015 (value 0.015, tol 1%) —
  matches.
