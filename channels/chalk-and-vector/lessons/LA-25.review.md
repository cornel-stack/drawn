## Equation `prEq`

`\boldsymbol{\pi} = H\,\boldsymbol{\pi}` — PageRank is the eigenvector of the
link matrix for eigenvalue 1. The source states the requirement directly (its
`Ⅰ` is our `π`, and it writes the product the other way round as `HⅠ=Ⅰ`):

> "With this notation, our requirements for the page rank are that HⅠ=Ⅰ. That
> is, we want an eigenvector of the matrix associated with the eigenvalue λ = 1."
> — Topic: Page Ranking, p.471

The circular-definition framing is also sourced:

> "a page is important if other important pages link to it ... But isn't that
> circular ... The answer is to use eigenvalues and eigenvectors." — p.470

Standard restatement with our symbol `π` for the importance vector. **ok.**

## Equation `linkEq`

`H_{ij} = 1/(\text{out-links of } j)` — each outgoing link carries an equal
share `1/aj` of the page's rank.

> "The increment depends on the importance of the linking page pj divided by how
> many out-links aj are on that page." — p.470
>
> "I(pi) = Σ_{in-linking pages pj} I(pj) / aj" — p.470

The worked matrix confirms the equal-share column structure: p3 has three
out-links so each receives `1/3`, p4 (dangling) is spread as `1/4` across all
pages. **ok** (restatement of the entry formula with explicit `1/aj`).

## Equation `steadyEq`

`\boldsymbol{\pi} = H\,\boldsymbol{\pi} \Leftrightarrow (H-I)\boldsymbol{\pi} = \mathbf{0}`
— the eigenvector/eigenvalue-1 condition rearranged.

> "our requirements for the page rank are that HⅠ=Ⅰ. That is, we want an
> eigenvector of the matrix associated with the eigenvalue λ = 1." — p.471

`Hπ = π ⟺ (H − I)π = 0` is the standard algebraic restatement of the λ=1
eigenvector condition. **ok.**

## Equation `dampEq`

`d = 0.85` — the damping factor (source calls it α).

> "In practice α is typically between 0.85 and 0.99." — p.472

The source's rank table also uses `α 0.85` as its first column, and the lesson
narrates "Google used eighty-five per cent" — the canonical Brin–Page value,
the low end of the stated range. **ok.**

## Equation `googleEq`

`G = d\,H + \dfrac{1-d}{N}\,J` — the Google matrix as a convex combination of
the link matrix and the uniform random-jump matrix.

> "G = α · [H] + (1 − α) · [S]  ... This is the Google matrix." — p.472
>
> "The Google matrix is computed by taking a combination G = α ∗ H + (1 − α) ∗ S
> of two stochastic matrices." — Exercise 1, p.472

The source's `S` is the all-`1/4` matrix, i.e. the uniform jump matrix with
every entry `1/N`; writing it as `(1/N)·J` (J the all-ones matrix) gives
exactly `(1−d)/N · J` with `d = α`. **ok** (notation `d`, `J`, `N` for the
source's `α`, uniform `S`, page count).
