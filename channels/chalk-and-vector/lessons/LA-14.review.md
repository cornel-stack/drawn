# Fact-check — LA-14

Primary source: Jim Hefferon, *Linear Algebra* (CC BY-SA 3.0), **Chapter Four
§II Geometry of Determinants** (local extract
`sources/text/hefferon-linear-algebra/027-geometry-of-determinants.md`, book
pp. 355-361) for the area-as-determinant formula, the determinant as the
size-scaling factor of a transformation, the sign-as-orientation reading, the
collapse-to-zero / singular case, the product theorem, and the lift to signed
volume in three dimensions. Both worked problems use our own 2x2 matrices
(numbers changed), adapted from §II Exercise 1.14 / Example 1.6 (Problem A, an
area stretch) and §II Remark 1.2's column-swap sign flip (Problem B, a reflecting
map).

## The central claims

**The 2x2 determinant is the (signed) area of the parallelogram, det A = ad - bc
(`formulaEq`, `detEq`).**

> "area of parallelogram = ... = x1 y2 - x2 y1 ... That the area equals the value
> of the determinant |x1 x2 ; y1 y2| = x1 y2 - x2 y1 is no coincidence."
> — §II.1, p. 355, lines 50-62

With columns (a,c) and (b,d) the signed area x1 y2 - x2 y1 is ad - bc, the 2x2
determinant. The interpretation of the determinant *value* as the size of the box
is explicit:

> "we are justified in interpreting det(v1, . . . , vn) as giving the size of the
> box formed by the vectors." — §II.1, p. 356-357, lines 99-100

**The determinant is the factor by which the map scales all area (`detEq`).**

> "1.5 Theorem A transformation t: Rn -> Rn changes the size of all boxes by the
> same factor, namely, the size of the image of a box |t(S)| is |T| times the size
> of the box |S|, where T is the matrix representing t with respect to the
> standard basis." — §II, Theorem 1.5, p. 358, lines 161-164

So |T| = det T is a single scaling factor applied to every region (the lesson's
"tile any region ... each is scaled by six"). Example 1.6 (p. 358, lines 188-216)
illustrates with a det-2 map that "will double sizes of boxes".

**A negative determinant means orientation is reversed/flipped (`signEq`
narration, `step2b`, `answer2`).**

> "Swapping the columns changes the sign. On the left, starting with u and
> following the arc inside the angle to v (that is, going counterclockwise), we
> get a positive size. On the right, ... following the clockwise arc, gives a
> negative size. The sign returned by the size function reflects the orientation
> or sense of the box." — §II.1, Remark 1.2, p. 357, lines 122-128

So positive det = orientation preserved, negative det = orientation flipped; the
magnitude is the area factor:

> "1.3 Definition The volume of a box is the absolute value of the determinant of
> a matrix with those vectors as columns." — §II.1, Definition 1.3, p. 357,
> lines 129-130

**det = 0 means the box is degenerate (zero area) / the map is singular (`signEq`
narration tail — "crushing the plane onto a line ... singular").**

> "First consider the case that T is singular and thus does not have an inverse.
> ... if T is not invertible then neither is TS — if |T| = 0 then |TS| = 0."
> — §II, Theorem 1.5 proof, p. 358, lines 172-175

> "Note that the vectors forming S make a linearly dependent set if and only if
> |S| = 0" — §II Exercise 1.25(a), p. 360, lines 462-463

A dependent set spans a flat (lower-dimensional) box of zero size, i.e. det 0
<=> degenerate box <=> singular (non-invertible). This matches the lesson's
"crushing the plane onto a line". **ok.**

**det(AB) = det(A) det(B) (`prodEq`).**

> "That is, the determinant of a product is the product of the determinants
> |TS| = |T| . |S|." — §II, Theorem 1.5, p. 358, lines 165-166

Proved by elementary-matrix factorisation on p. 358, lines 167-187. **ok.**

**In three dimensions the determinant is the signed volume (lesson's 3D lift).**

> "1.3 Definition The volume of a box is the absolute value of the determinant of
> a matrix with those vectors as columns." — §II.1, Definition 1.3, p. 357,
> lines 129-130

> "1.4 Example By the formula that takes the area of the base times the height,
> the volume of this parallelepiped is 12. That agrees with the determinant
> |2 0 2 ; 0 3 1 ; -1 0 1| = 12. Taking the vectors in a different order changes
> the sign but not the magnitude ... = -12." — §II.1, Example 1.4, p. 357,
> lines 131-157

So in R3 the determinant is the signed volume of the parallelepiped (box), with
the sign distinguishing left- from right-handed orientation. **ok.**

## Problem s4 — area scaling of A = [[3,1],[0,2]] (`given`, `step1`, `answer`, check)

Our own matrix, adapted from §II Example 1.6 / Exercise 1.14(a) (p. 358-359).
det = 3*2 - 1*0 = 6 > 0 (`step1`), so the area factor is 6 and, being positive,
orientation is preserved (`answer`). The gate re-runs `check.expr` `3*2 - 1*0`,
declared `value: 6`, tol 1%.

```python
import numpy as np
A = np.array([[3,1],[0,2]], float)
detA_formula = 3*2 - 1*0
print("det A = 3*2 - 1*0   =", detA_formula)
print("np.linalg.det(A)    =", round(np.linalg.det(A), 12))
print("sign                =", "positive, orientation preserved" if np.linalg.det(A) > 0 else "flip")
print("|det A| (area factor)=", abs(detA_formula))
print("s4 check '3*2 - 1*0'=", 3*2 - 1*0)
```

output:
```
det A = 3*2 - 1*0   = 6
np.linalg.det(A)    = 6.0
sign                = positive, orientation preserved
|det A| (area factor)= 6
s4 check '3*2 - 1*0'= 6
```

det 6 > 0: area is multiplied by 6, orientation preserved. `check.expr` returns
6 = `value`. **Verdict: ok.**

## Problem s5 — negative determinant of B = [[0,2],[3,0]] (`given2`, `step1b`, `step2b`, `answer2`, check)

Our own matrix, adapted from §II Remark 1.2's column-swap sign flip (p. 357).
det = 0*0 - 2*3 = -6 < 0 (`step1b`, `step2b`), so the area factor is |det| = 6 and
the negative sign flags a reflected (flipped) orientation (`answer2`). The gate

```
|det B| (area factor)= 6
s5 check '0*0 - 2*3'= -6
```

det -6 < 0: area factor |det| = 6, orientation flipped (reflection). `check.expr`
returns -6 = `value`. **Verdict: ok.**

## Not from Hefferon (our own numbers)

Both worked problems use our own 2x2 matrices — s4: A = [[3,1],[0,2]] (det 6,
area x6, orientation preserved); s5: B = [[0,2],[3,0]] (det -6, area x6,
orientation flipped). These are not Hefferon's worked examples (adapted from §II
Example 1.6 / Exercise 1.14 and Remark 1.2). The area = det formula (§II.1
p. 355), the determinant-as-size-scaling-factor theorem (Theorem 1.5, p. 358),
the sign-as-orientation reading (Remark 1.2, p. 357), the degenerate-box /
singular case (Theorem 1.5 proof p. 358, Exercise 1.25(a) p. 360), the product
theorem det(AB) = det(A)det(B) (Theorem 1.5, p. 358), and the signed-volume lift
to R3 (Definition 1.3 / Example 1.4, p. 357) are all Hefferon's, applied to our
numbers.
