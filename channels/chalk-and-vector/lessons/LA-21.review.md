## Equation `charEq`  `det(A - λI) = 0`

> "The equation⃗0 = (λI−T)⃗v = (I −T)⃗v is"
> — 039-topic-stable-populations.md:33

> "We consider λ⃗v = T⃗v and solve for λ. 0 = | λ −.9 / .01 / .10 / λ −.99 |"
> — 039-topic-stable-populations.md:85-88

Eigenvalues are the λ for which `(λI−T)v=0` has a non-zero solution, i.e. the
matrix `λI−T` (equivalently `A−λI`, differing only by overall sign) is singular,
so its determinant vanishes: `det(A−λI)=0`. The source sets the determinant
to 0 and solves for λ. Standard identity, consistent with source.

## Equation `polyEq`  `λ² - tr(A)λ + det(A) = 0`

> "= (λ −.9)(λ −.99) −(.10)(.01) = λ2 −1.89λ + .89"

The diagonal entries here are −.9 and −.99, giving λ-coefficient
−(−.9 −.99) = −1.89 = −tr and constant (.9)(.99)−(.01)(.10)=.89=det. This is
exactly `λ² − tr λ + det`. Standard identity, consistent with source.
