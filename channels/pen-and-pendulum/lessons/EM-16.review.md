## Equation `emfEq`

`ε = -L dI/dt` — self-inductance / Faraday's law form.

> "In accordance with Lenz's law, the negative sign in Equation 14.10 indicates
> that the induced emf across an inductor always has a polarity that opposes the
> change in the current."
> — University Physics Vol. 2, §14.2 Self-Inductance and Inductors, Eq 14.10, p.596

Note: the display equation ε = −L dI/dt is Eq 14.10 (rendered as placeholder
"14.10" in the extract); the prose above names it and fixes its sign and form.
Verdict ok.

## Equation `induEq`

`L = N Φ_m / I` — flux linkage per unit current.

> "With the flux known, the self-inductance can be found from Equation 14.9…"
> and "If the loop has N turns, this equation becomes [N Φ = L I]"
> — University Physics Vol. 2, §14.2, Eq 14.9, p.595 and p.598

Note: Eq 14.9 is NΦ = LI, i.e. L = NΦ_m/I; rendered as placeholder "14.9" in the
extract but the N-turns flux-linkage relation is stated in prose (p.595, lines
27-28). Verdict ok.

## Equation `kvlEq`

`ε = IR + L dI/dt` — loop rule for the series RL circuit.

> "Applying Kirchhoff's loop rule to this circuit, we obtain [ε = IR + L dI/dt]
> which is a first-order differential equation for I(t)."
> — University Physics Vol. 2, §14.4 RL Circuits, Eq 14.23, p.603

Note: display equation is Eq 14.23 (placeholder "14.23"); the surrounding prose
states it is the loop rule giving the first-order ODE for I(t). Verdict ok.

## Equation `riseEq`

`I(t) = (ε/R)(1 - e^{-t/τ})` — current rise in an RL circuit.

> "the solution to Equation 14.23 can be found by making substitutions in the
> equations relating the capacitor to the inductor. This gives [I(t)]…"
> and "It starts at zero, and as t→∞, I(t) approaches [ε/R] asymptotically."
> — University Physics Vol. 2, §14.4, Eq 14.24, p.603

Note: Eq 14.24 is I(t) = (ε/R)(1 − e^{−t/τ}) (placeholder "14.24"); prose
confirms start at 0 and asymptote ε/R. Example 14.4 (p.605) applies exactly this
form. Verdict ok.

## Equation `workEq`

`U = ∫_0^I L i di` — energy as the integral of the power the inductor absorbs.

> "At any instant, the magnitude of the induced emf is [L di/dt] … the power
> absorbed by the inductor is [L i di/dt]. The total energy stored in the
> magnetic field when the current increases from 0 to I … can be determined by
> integrating this expression."
> — University Physics Vol. 2, §14.3 Energy in a Magnetic Field, Eqs 14.20-14.22, p.600

Note: P = Li(di/dt) integrated over time from 0 to I gives U = ∫_0^I L i di
(= ½LI²). The integrand and limits are stated in prose; Eq 14.22 (placeholder)
is the resulting U = ½LI². Verdict ok.

## Equation `energyEq`

`U = ½ L I²` — energy stored in the magnetic field.

> "The energy stored in the magnetic field of an inductor is [U = ½ L I²]."
> — University Physics Vol. 2, §14.4, Eq 14.26, p.604 (derived in §14.3, Eq 14.22, p.600)

Also §14.3: "Although derived for a special case, this equation gives the energy
stored in the magnetic field of any inductor." Note: display form is Eq 14.22 /
restated 14.26 (placeholders); prose states U = ½LI² for any inductor. Verdict ok.
