# Fact-check — EM-21

OpenStax *University Physics Volume 2*, §16.2 Plane Electromagnetic Waves
(CC BY 4.0), PDF pp. 676–682 (printed pp. 664–669). Source file:
`sources/text/university-physics-vol2/140-16-2-plane-electromagnetic-waves.md`.

Note on the source text: the extracted markdown renders the numbered equation
**bodies** as placeholders (the equation-number labels — 16.13 … 16.22 — appear
on their own lines, the LaTeX did not survive extraction). Each equation is
therefore verified against the **surrounding prose that names what it says**,
plus its equation number, as the house rules permit. The section performs
exactly the derivation this lesson teaches: Faraday + Ampère-Maxwell →
∂²E/∂x² = μ₀ε₀ ∂²E/∂t² → match to the standard wave equation → c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀),
and separately E = cB.

## Equation `waveEq`

∂²E/∂x² = μ₀ε₀ ∂²E/∂t² (scene s2, identical body to `waveEq2`). Eq 16.20.

> "We next combine the equations showing the changing B field producing an E
> field with the equation showing the changing E field producing a B field.
> Taking the derivative of Equation 16.16 with respect to x and using Equation
> 16.19 gives [Eq 16.20] … This is the form taken by the general wave equation
> for our plane wave." — §16.2, Eq 16.20, p.667

The combination (derivative of the Faraday relation + substitution of the
Ampère-Maxwell relation) yields exactly ∂²E/∂x² = μ₀ε₀ ∂²E/∂t².

## Equation `faradayEq`

∂E/∂x = −∂B/∂t (scene s3). Eq 16.16, from applying Faraday's law.

> "First, we apply Faraday's law over Side 3 of the Gaussian surface … Canceling
> … and taking the limit as [Δx → 0], we are left with [Eq 16.16] … This is the
> equation describing the spatially dependent E field produced by the
> time-dependent B field." — §16.2, Eq 16.15/16.16, p.666

Sign: the standard orientation (E in y, B in z, wave along +x) gives
∂Eᵧ/∂x = −∂B_z/∂t. The lesson writes the one-component form ∂E/∂x = −∂B/∂t,
the standard form for this geometry. The source states the law and geometry
(Faraday's law applied along Side 3 of Figure 16.7) that produce this sign; the
placeholder body hides the explicit minus, but the convention matches
(Faraday's law = negative rate of change of flux).

## Equation `ampereEq`

∂B/∂x = −μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t (scene s3). Eq 16.17/16.19, from the Ampère-Maxwell law.

> "Next we apply the Ampère-Maxwell law (with [J = 0]) over the same two faces
> (Surface 3 and then Surface 2) … yields the two equations [Eq 16.17, 16.19].
> These equations describe the spatially dependent B field produced by the
> time-dependent E field." — §16.2, Eq 16.17/16.19, p.666

Sign and coefficient: the Ampère-Maxwell law in free space (no current) is
∮B·dl = μ₀ε₀ dΦ_E/dt, giving ∂B_z/∂x = −μ₀ε₀ ∂Eᵧ/∂t for this geometry — the
coefficient μ₀ε₀ and the structure (space-change of B tied to time-change of E)
match the source's described relation. The lesson's one-component form is the
standard form.

## Equation `waveEq2`

∂²E/∂x² = μ₀ε₀ ∂²E/∂t² (scene s3, boxed). Same as Eq 16.20 — see `waveEq`.

> "Taking the derivative of Equation 16.16 with respect to x and using Equation
> 16.19 gives [Eq 16.20]. This is the form taken by the general wave equation
> for our plane wave." — §16.2, Eq 16.20, p.667

## Equation `standardEq`

∂²E/∂x² = (1/c²) ∂²E/∂t² (scene s3). Eq 16.21, the standard wave equation.

> "Because the equations describe a wave traveling at some as-yet-unspecified
> speed c, we can assume the field components are each functions of x – ct …
> It is left as a mathematical exercise to show, using the chain rule for
> differentiation, that Equation 16.17 and Equation 16.18 imply [Eq 16.21]."
> — §16.2, Eq 16.21, p.667

Matching Eq 16.20 (coefficient μ₀ε₀) to Eq 16.21 (coefficient 1/c²) forces
μ₀ε₀ = 1/c², exactly the lesson's argument.

## Equation `speedEq`

c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) (scene s3, boxed). Eq 16.18/16.22.

> "The speed of the electromagnetic wave in free space is therefore given in
> terms of the permeability and the permittivity of free space by [Eq 16.18]."
> — §16.2, Eq 16.18, p.667

> "If we evaluate the speed [c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)] we find that [≈ 3×10⁸ m/s] which is
> the speed of light." — §16.2, Eq 16.22, p.667

## Problem `s4`

```python
B0 = 2e-6    # T, magnetic amplitude
E0 = c * B0  # E = cB
print(E0, "V/m")
# check.expr:
print(3e8 * 2e-6)
```

output:
```

600.0
```

Boxed answer `E0 = 600 V/m` matches; `check.expr` (3e8*2e-6) = 600.0, value 600,
tol 1% → agrees.
