## Equation `bsEq` — dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl × r̂)/r² (scene s3)

§12.1, Eq **12.1** (p.502, PDF p.514), quoted:

> "The Biot-Savart law states that at any point P (Figure 12.2), the magnetic
> field due to an element [dl] of a current-carrying wire is given by"

(the displayed **dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl × r̂)/r²** sat on the blank line immediately
after — Eq 12.1). **Cross product / perpendicular / inverse-square**, quoted:

> "The direction of [dB] is determined by applying the right-hand rule to the
> vector product [dl × r̂]."
> "Notice that if [θ = 0] then [dB = 0]. The field produced by a current element
> [I dl] has no component parallel to [dl]."

(the r² denominator is the inverse-square dependence; θ is the angle between dl
and r̂, so the field is largest at 90° and curls around the current, never
along it). **μ₀**, quoted:

> "The constant [μ₀] is known as the permeability of free space and is exactly
> [4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A] in the SI system."

The board's `d\vec{B} = (μ₀/4π)(I d\vec{l}×\hat{r})/r²` with say "d B equals mu
nought over four pi, times I d l cross r hat, over r squared" matches exactly,
and the narration's "proportional to the current and the length of the element…
falls off as the inverse square of the distance… direction is given by a cross
product… so the field curls around the current, never points along it… mu
nought over four pi, carries the permeability of free space" is the source in
words. ✔

## Equation `wireEq` / `wireEq2` — B = μ₀I/(2πr) (scenes s2, s3)

§12.2, Eq **12.7** (p.506, PDF p.519). The derivation starts from the
Biot-Savart law (Eq 12.5, quoted: "With [dl × r̂ = …] we have from the
Biot-Savart law"), integrates 0→∞ and doubles ("The wire is symmetrical about
point O, so we can set the limits of the integration from zero to infinity and
double the answer"), and closes:

> "Substituting the limits gives us the solution"

(the displayed **B = μ₀I/(2πR)** sat on the blank line after — Eq 12.7). **1/r
decline and circling direction**, quoted:

> "The magnetic field lines of the infinite wire are circular and centered at the
> wire (Figure 12.6)… Since the field decreases with distance from the wire, the
> spacing of the field lines must increase correspondingly with distance."

**Right-hand rule for direction**, quoted (RHR-2):

> "The direction of this magnetic field may be found with a second form of the
> right-hand rule… If you hold the wire with your right hand so that your thumb
> points along the current, then your fingers wrap around the wire in the same
> sense as [B]."

The board `wireEq` B = μ₀I/(2πr) (legend I [A], r [m], μ₀) and the boxed
`wireEq2` B_{wire} = μ₀I/(2πr) both match Eq 12.7 exactly. The narration's
"falling off as one over the distance… Double your distance from the wire and
the field halves; go ten times further and it is a tenth as strong… circling
the wire" is the inverse-first-power behaviour of Eq 12.7 in words. ✔ (The
narration's observation that the geometry turns the per-element inverse-square
into an inverse-first-power for the whole line is the standard consequence of
integrating Eq 12.5 along the infinite line — physically correct, and the result
is the quoted Eq 12.7.) ✔

## Equation `loopEq` — B = μ₀I/(2R) at the centre (scene s3)

§12.4, Eq **12.16** (p.512, PDF p.524). On-axis field (Eq 12.15) derived from
the Biot-Savart law with the perpendicular components cancelling in pairs,
quoted:

> "the net magnetic field direction is along the central axis from the loop."
> "The components of [dB₁] and [dB₂] perpendicular to the y-axis therefore
> cancel… The components perpendicular to the axis of the loop sum to zero in
> pairs."

and the centre result:

> "By setting [y = 0] in Equation 12.15, we obtain the magnetic field at the
> center of the loop:"

(the displayed **B = μ₀I/(2R)** sat on the blank line after — Eq 12.16). The
board `loopEq` B_{loop} = μ₀I/(2R) matches exactly (N=1, z=0). The narration's
"Every element of the ring is the same distance R away, and by the cross product
each contributes field in the same direction, straight through the centre, so
they reinforce rather than partly cancel" is the source's cancel-in-pairs /
axial-reinforcement argument in words. ✔ (The coil/solenoid aside — "stack a
hundred such loops… the field multiplies a hundredfold" — is the source's own
"This equation becomes [B = μ₀NI/(2R)] for a flat coil of N loops", Eq 12.17,
p.512.) ✔

## Comparison with the electric field (narration, scene s3)

The narration's "Both fall off as the inverse square from a point source, and
both carry a constant of nature out front. But the electric field of a charge
points straight out along the line to it, while the magnetic field of a current
element points sideways, at right angles, because of the cross product" is a
standard, physically-correct comparison. The source motivates exactly this
parallel in §12.1: "We have seen that mass produces a gravitational field…
Charge produces an electric field… Since moving charge (that is, current)
interacts with a magnetic field, we might expect that it also creates that
field—and it does." The sideways/cross-product distinction follows directly from
the quoted Eq 12.1 vector product. Marked **standard**, consistent with the
quoted law. ✔

## Problem `s4` — field near a straight wire (equations `given`, `step1`, `answer`)

Self-contained application of §12.2 Eq 12.7, B = μ₀I/(2πr), with
μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A (§12.1). Numbers are our own: I=10 A, r=0.05 m.
Recomputation:

```python
import numpy as np
mu0 = 4*np.pi*1e-7
I, r = 10, 0.05
B = (mu0*I)/(2*np.pi*r)
print("B = mu0*I/(2*pi*r) =", B, "T")
print("check expr (4*np.pi*1e-7 * 10)/(2*np.pi*0.05) =", (4*np.pi*1e-7*10)/(2*np.pi*0.05))
print("boxed value 4e-5; rel diff =", abs(B-4e-5)/4e-5)
```

Output:

```
B = mu0*I/(2*pi*r) = 4e-05 T
check expr (4*np.pi*1e-7 * 10)/(2*np.pi*0.05) = 4e-05
boxed value 4e-5; rel diff = 0.0
```

Board `step1` substitutes (4π×10⁻⁷)(10)/(2π·0.05) — the π cancels — and `answer`
boxes **4×10⁻⁵ T**, matching to 0.00 %. Units: (T·m/A)(A)/m = T. ✔
