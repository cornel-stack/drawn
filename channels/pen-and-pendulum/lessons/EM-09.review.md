# Fact-check — EM-09 (The magnetic force on a moving charge and why it does no work)

Source: OpenStax *University Physics Volume 2* (CC BY 4.0), §11.2 *Magnetic
Fields and Lines* (book pp. 466–469, PDF pp. 478–481) and §11.3 *Motion of a
Charged Particle in a Magnetic Field* (book pp. 470–473, PDF pp. 482–485).

`sources/text/university-physics-vol2/092-11-2-magnetic-fields-and-lines.md` and

## Equation `forceEq` — F = q v × B

§11.2, Defining the Magnetic Field, Eq **11.1** (p.466, PDF p.478), quoted:

> "Based on these observations, we define the magnetic field strength B based on
> the magnetic force on a charge q moving at velocity [v] as the cross product of
> the velocity and magnetic field, that is,"

(the displayed **F = q v × B** sat on the blank line after "that is,"), with the
direction statement, same section (p.466):

> "The direction of this force is perpendicular to both the direction of the
> moving charged particle and the direction of the applied magnetic field."

The board's `\vec{F} = q\,\vec{v}\times\vec{B}` with legend q [C], v [m/s], B [T]
matches exactly; the "perpendicular to both" character stated in the narration is
quoted verbatim. The **right-hand rule** (narration) is the source's RHR-1,
Problem-Solving Strategy p.466 (PDF p.478):

> "1. Orient your right hand so that your fingers curl in the plane defined by the
> velocity and magnetic field vectors. 2. …sweep from the velocity toward the
> magnetic field… 3. The magnetic force is directed where your thumb is pointing.
> 4. If the charge was negative, reverse the direction found by these steps."

matching the narration's "point your fingers along v, curl them toward B, and
your thumb gives the force on a positive charge… Reverse the charge's sign… and
the force flips." ✔

## Equation `magEq` — F = qvB sin θ

§11.2, Defining the Magnetic Field, Eq **11.2** (p.466, PDF p.478), quoted:

> "In other words, the magnitude of the force satisfies [F = qvB sin θ] where θ is
> the angle between the velocity and the magnetic field."

(the displayed **F = qvB sin θ** sat on the blank line before "where θ is the
angle"). Maximum at θ=90° and zero for v∥B is the source's own Example 11.1(c)
(p.468, PDF p.480):

> "Since the velocity and magnetic field are parallel to each other, there is no
> orientation of your hand that will result in a force direction. Therefore, the
> force on this moving charge is zero. This is confirmed by the cross product.
> When you cross two vectors pointing in the same direction, the result is equal
> to zero."

The board's `F = qvB\sin\theta` is identical; the narration's "largest when the
charge moves across the field, at ninety degrees, and exactly zero when it moves
straight along the field" matches the source. ✔

## Equation `workEq` — W = 0 (F ⊥ v)

§11.3, p.470 (PDF p.482), quoted:

> "Since the magnetic force is perpendicular to the direction of travel, a charged
> particle follows a curved path in a magnetic field. … Another way to look at
> this is that the magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity, so that it
> does no work on the charged particle. The particle's kinetic energy and speed
> thus remain constant. The direction of motion is affected but not the speed."

The board's `W = 0 \quad (\vec{F} \perp \vec{v})` states exactly this. The
narration's reasoning — "Work is force times displacement along the motion, and
the magnetic force has no component along the motion, because it is perpendicular
to the velocity. So the work is zero, the kinetic energy never changes, and the
speed stays constant" — is the standard definition W = F·d applied to the
source's "perpendicular to velocity → does no work → kinetic energy and speed
remain constant." ✔

## Equation `radiusEq` — r = mv/(qB)

§11.3, Eq **11.4** (p.470–471, PDF p.482–483), quoted:

> "In this situation, the magnetic force supplies the centripetal force [F =
> mv²/r]. Noting that the velocity is perpendicular to the magnetic field, the
> magnitude of the magnetic force is reduced to [F = qvB]. Because the magnetic
> force F supplies the centripetal force… we have [qvB = mv²/r]. Solving for r
> yields [r = mv/(qB)]."

(the displayed equations sat on the blank lines; the label, same page: "Here, r
is the radius of curvature of the path of a charged particle with mass m and
charge q, moving at a speed v that is perpendicular to a magnetic field of
strength B."). The board's `r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}`, derived on the board from qvB =
mv²/r exactly as the source does, matches. The narration's "Faster or heavier
particles circle wider; stronger fields and bigger charges rein them into tighter
loops" is the correct reading of r ∝ mv and r ∝ 1/(qB). ✔

## Helical motion and applications (narration)

**Helix** when v has a component along B — §11.3, Eq 11.7 (p.471, PDF p.483),
quoted:

> "If the velocity is not perpendicular to the magnetic field, then we can compare
> each component of the velocity separately with the magnetic field. The component
> of the velocity perpendicular to the magnetic field produces a magnetic force
> perpendicular to both this velocity and the field… The component parallel to the
> magnetic field creates constant motion along the same direction as the magnetic
> field… The result is a helical motion."

matching the narration's "the across part circles while the along part coasts
untouched, so the path becomes a helix." ✔

**Auroras** — §11.3, p.472 (PDF p.484), quoted:

> "Aurorae, like the famous aurora borealis (northern lights)… are beautiful
> displays of light emitted as ions recombine with electrons entering the
> atmosphere as they spiral along magnetic field lines."

matching the narration's "charged particles from the Sun are funnelled down
Earth's magnetic field toward the poles to light the auroras." ✔

**Mass spectrometer / particle accelerators** (narration "how a mass spectrometer
sorts ions by their mass, and how the great particle accelerators bend beams
around their rings"): not stated verbatim in §11.2–11.3, but a direct, standard
application of r = mv/(qB) (§11.4 covers mass spectrometer and velocity selector;
§11.7 covers cyclotrons in this chapter). The narration's reservation that
electric fields do the accelerating while magnets only steer is a correct
consequence of the "magnetic force does no work" result quoted above — marked
**standard**, consistent with the source. ✔

## Problem `s4` — force magnitude (equations `given`, `step1`, `answer`)

Self-contained application of §11.2 Eq 11.2, F = qvB sin θ with θ=90° (sin θ = 1),
so F = qvB. Numbers are our own: q = 2 C, v = 3 m/s, B = 4 T — deliberately large
everyday values, a didactic choice (not a real particle, whose q would be
~10⁻¹⁹ C). Recomputation:

```python
import math
q, v, B, theta = 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 90.0
F = q * v * B * math.sin(math.radians(theta))
print("F = qvB sin(theta) = 2*3*4*sin90 =", F, "N")
```

Output:

```
F = qvB sin(theta) = 2*3*4*sin90 = 24.0 N
```

Board `step1` substitutes (2)(3)(4); `answer` boxes **24 N** — matches to 0.00 %.
Units: C·(m/s)·T = C·(m/s)·(kg/(C·s)) = kg·m/s² = N. ✔

## Problem `s5` — radius of the circle (equations `given2`, `step2`, `answer2`)

Self-contained application of §11.3 Eq 11.4, r = mv/(qB). Numbers are our own:
m = 0.8 kg, v = 3 m/s, q = 2 C, B = 4 T — again deliberately large everyday
values, a didactic choice (not a real particle). Recomputation:

```python
m, v, q, B = 0.8, 3.0, 2.0, 4.0
r = m * v / (q * B)
print("r = mv/(qB) = (0.8*3)/(2*4) =", r, "m")
```

Output:

```
r = mv/(qB) = (0.8*3)/(2*4) = 0.30000000000000004 m
```

Board `step2` substitutes (0.8)(3)/((2)(4)); `answer2` boxes **0.3 m** (via
2.4/8) — matches to 0.00 %. Units: kg·(m/s)/(C·T) = kg·(m/s)/(C·kg/(C·s)) =
(m/s)·s = m. ✔
