## Equation `emfEq` — ε = BLv (scene s2)

§13.3, Eq **13.5** (p.555, PDF p.567), quoted:

> "Since B and l are constant and the velocity of the rod is [v = dx/dt] we can
> now restate Faraday's law, Equation 13.2, for the magnitude of the emf in terms
> of the moving conducting rod as [ε = Blv]."

(the displayed **ε = Blv** sat on the blank line after "the moving conducting rod
as" — Eq 13.5). The board's `ε = BLv` matches Eq 13.5 exactly (L = source's l).
The §13.3 learning objective frames the whole lesson: "Determine the magnitude of
an induced emf in a wire moving at a constant speed through a magnetic field." The
s2 narration — a rod of free charges carried at velocity v feels F = qv×B along
its length, charge gathers at the ends, a voltage builds "exactly as a battery
maintains a voltage across its terminals", and "the faster the rod moves … the
larger the voltage" — is the ε ∝ v content of Eq 13.5 in words, with the force
origin F = qv×B (Ch. 11). ✔

## Equation `fluxWay` — Φ = BLx ⇒ ε = dΦ/dt = BLv (scene s3, derivation 1)

§13.3, Eq **13.4 → 13.5** (p.555, PDF p.567), quoted:

> "Now let's look at a conducting rod pulled in a circuit, changing magnetic flux.
> The area enclosed by the circuit 'MNOP' of Figure 13.12 is lx and is
> perpendicular to the magnetic field, so we can simplify the integration of
> Equation 13.1 into a multiplication of magnetic field and area. The magnetic
> flux through the open surface is therefore [Φ = Blx]."

(displayed **Φ = Blx** on the blank line — Eq 13.4), then

> "Since B and l are constant and the velocity of the rod is [v = dx/dt] we can
> now restate Faraday's law, Equation 13.2 … as [ε = Blv]." (Eq 13.5)

The imaginary-loop restatement confirms the explicit differentiation (p.556, PDF
p.568):

> "The area of the rectangle is lx, so the magnetic flux through it is [Φ = Blx].
> Differentiating this equation, we obtain [ε = dΦ/dt = Blv] which is identical to
> the potential difference between the ends of the rod that we determined earlier."

The board's `Φ = BLx ⇒ ε = dΦ/dt = BLv` is Eq 13.4 differentiated into Eq 13.5
exactly (magnitude form; the −sign is direction, deferred to Lenz's law — §13.3:
"the direction of the induced emf satisfies Lenz's law"). The narration — rod on
rails a distance L apart, flux = B·area = BLx, rate of change = BL·(dx/dt) = BLv
— is this derivation step by step. ✔

## Equation `forceWay` — qE = qvB ⇒ E = vB (scene s3, derivation 2)

§13.3, Example 13.5 Significance (p.559, PDF p.571), gives the second (integrate-
in-space / force) route:

> "An alternative way of looking at the induced emf from Faraday's law is to
> integrate in space instead of time. The solution, however, would be the same.
> The motional emf is [ε = ∫(v×B)·dl]."

The §13.3 generality statement (p.555, PDF p.567):

> "With [ε = ∫(v×B)·dl] as the starting point, it can be shown that [ε = −dΦ/dt]
> holds for any change in flux caused by the motion of a conductor."

The microscopic content is the magnetic force on a moving charge, F = qv×B (Ch.
11 §11.2). Each free charge in the rod feels qvB along its length; charge
separates until the electrostatic field it creates cancels the magnetic push:
`qE = qvB ⇒ E = vB`. Integrating E over the rod length L gives ε = EL = vBL, the
uniform-case reduction of ε = ∫(v×B)·dl. The board's `qE = qvB ⇒ E = vB` is this
force balance, and the narration ("charge keeps flowing until that electric field
exactly cancels the magnetic push") states it precisely. The source explicitly
asserts the two routes agree: "The solution, however, would be the same." ✔

## Equation `emfEq2` — ε = EL = BLv (scene s3, boxed result)

§13.3, Eq **13.5** (p.555, PDF p.567) — the identical boxed result ε = Blv reached
by the second route. From `forceWay`, E = vB; the emf is this field times the rod
length, ε = EL = (vB)L = **BLv**, matching Eq 13.5. The source confirms the two
derivations land on the same value (Example 13.5 Significance: "The solution,
however, would be the same"; §13.3 body: "which is identical to the potential
difference between the ends of the rod that we determined earlier"). The
narration's claim — two arguments from different starting points (changing area
vs. force on charges) meeting at the identical result, and that the flux rule is
"a bookkeeping summary of the force on moving charges" — is directly supported by
§13.3's "Thus Faraday's law holds for all flux changes, whether they are produced
by a changing magnetic field, by motion, or by a combination of the two." The
relativity/Maxwell commentary is standard physics context, framed as motivation,
not asserted as a §13.3 result. Board's boxed `ε = EL = BLv` matches Eq 13.5. ✔

## Energy / retarding-force narration (scenes s3, s5)

§13.3, p.556 (PDF p.568), quoted:

> "From an energy perspective, [ε = Blv] produces power [P = εI] and the resistor
> dissipates power [P = I²R]. Since the rod is moving at constant velocity, the
> applied force [Fapp] must balance the magnetic force [Fmag] on the rod when it
> is carrying the induced current I … In satisfying the principle of energy
> conservation, the produced and dissipated powers are equal."

This underwrites the narration "push it along and it pumps out current, resisting
you with a force you feel as the price of the electricity it makes" (s3) and "feels
a retarding force that opposes the rod's motion — Lenz's law again — so you must
keep pushing … and the work you do is exactly the electrical energy delivered to
the resistor" (s5). ✔

## Problem `s4` — motional emf of a sliding rod (equations `given`, `step1`, `answer`)


## Problem `s5` — current driven through a resistor (equations `given2`, `step2`, `answer2`)

balance quoted above. ✔
