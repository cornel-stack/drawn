# Fact-check — EM-10 (Torque on a current loop in a magnetic field: how a motor works)

Source: OpenStax *University Physics Volume 2* (CC BY 4.0), §11.5 *Force and
Torque on a Current Loop* (book pp. 479–480, PDF pp. 491–492). Extracted text:
`sources/text/university-physics-vol2/095-11-5-force-and-torque-on-a-current-loop.md`.

> Note on quoting: the OpenStax PDF renders its displayed (numbered) equations as
> images, so the symbolic glyphs are absent from the extracted text — only the
> surrounding prose that introduces or labels each one survives (same situation
> as `EM-06`–`EM-09.review.md`). Where that is so, the quoted line is the
> sentence that defines or labels the equation and the symbolic form is confirmed
> against the standard OpenStax equation number for this section: Eq **11.14** the
> force on a straight current-carrying wire (F = I l × B), Eq **11.18** the vector
> dipole moment definition (μ = I A n̂), Eq **11.19** the torque τ = NIAB sin θ,
> Eq **11.20** the N-turn moment μ = NIA n̂, Eq **11.21** the compact form
> τ = μ × B. The blank lines in the extracted text are exactly where the
> image-equations sat (e.g. after "length l is given by", after "This simplifies
> to", after "defined as", after "can be written").

## Equation `crossEq` — τ = μ × B (scene s2)

§11.5, Eq **11.21** (p.480, PDF p.492), quoted:

> "In terms of the magnetic dipole moment, the torque on a current loop due to a
> uniform magnetic field can be written"

(the displayed **τ = μ × B** sat on the blank line immediately after). The board's
`\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}` with legend μ [A·m²], B [T] matches exactly.
As a cross product the torque is perpendicular to both the moment and the field,
and it drives the moment toward alignment with B — matching the narration's "the
torque is perpendicular to both the moment and the field, and it acts to rotate
the loop so that its moment swings into line with the field." ✔

## Equation `forceSides` — F = BIL on each side (scene s3)

§11.5, Eq **11.14** (p.479, PDF p.491), quoted:

> "The magnetic force on a straight current-carrying wire of length l is given by"

(the displayed **F = I l × B** sat on the blank line after; with l ⊥ B the
magnitude is F = BIL). Applied to the loop's sides, same section (p.479):

> "To find the net force on the loop, we have to apply this equation to each of
> the four sides. The force on side 1 is [F₁]… where the direction has been
> determined with the RHR-1. The current in side 3 flows in the opposite
> direction to that of side 1, so [F₃ = −F₁]."

So two opposite sides feel equal-and-opposite forces of size BIL. The board's
`F = BIL (on each side)` matches; the narration's "Each side carries current
across the field and feels a force of size B I L. On two opposite sides the
currents run in opposite directions, so the forces point opposite ways" is the
source verbatim in words. ✔

## Equation `torqueEq` — τ = NIAB sin θ (scene s3)

§11.5, Eq **11.19** (p.480, PDF p.492). The couple that produces it, quoted
(p.479–480):

> "Since they have the same line of action and are equal and opposite, the sum of
> their torques about any axis is zero… Thus, if there is any torque on the loop,
> it must be furnished by [the forces on the other two sides]."

and the result:

> "This simplifies to [τ = NIAB sin θ] where [A = ab] is the area of the loop."

(the displayed Eq sat on the blank line after "This simplifies to"). The board's
`\tau = NIAB\sin\theta` matches exactly. The narration's "a couple is a pure
torque: it does not shove the loop anywhere, it only spins it… the number of
turns, times the current, times the loop's area, times the field, times the sine
of the angle" is the source's couple argument and Eq 11.19 in words. **Maximum at
θ=90° / zero when faced square-on** is Eq 11.19 itself (sin θ): the narration's
"greatest when the loop lies edge-on to the field and vanishes when it faces the
field square on" — i.e. max when μ ⊥ B (θ=90°), zero when μ ∥ B (θ=0°). ✔

## Equation `momentEq` — μ = NIA (scene s3)

§11.5, Eqs **11.18** and **11.20** (p.480, PDF p.492), quoted:

> "A closed-current loop is commonly referred to as a magnetic dipole and the term
> IA is known as its magnetic dipole moment [μ]. Actually, the magnetic dipole
> moment is a vector that is defined as [μ = I A n̂] where n̂ is a unit vector
> directed perpendicular to the plane of the loop… The direction of [μ] is
> obtained with the RHR-2—if you curl the fingers of your right hand in the
> direction of current flow in the loop, then your thumb points along [μ]. If the
> loop contains N turns of wire, then its magnetic dipole moment is given by
> [μ = NIA n̂]."

The board's `\mu = NIA` matches Eq 11.20 (magnitude). **Direction perpendicular
to the loop by the right-hand rule** is quoted verbatim (RHR-2), matching the
narration's "pointing straight out of the face of the loop" and "A current loop
behaves like a tiny bar magnet, and this one vector captures it." ✔

## Equation `crossEq2` — τ = μ × B, boxed compact form (scene s3)

§11.5, Eq **11.21** (p.480, PDF p.492) — same quote as `crossEq`:

> "In terms of the magnetic dipole moment, the torque on a current loop due to a
> uniform magnetic field can be written"

(the displayed **τ = μ × B** sat on the blank line after). The board's boxed
`\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}` matches exactly — the compact form that
bundles N, I, A into μ. ✔

## Commutator / DC motor (narration, scene s3)

Stated **verbatim** in §11.5 (p.479, PDF p.491), quoted:

> "Motors are the most common application of magnetic force on current-carrying
> wires. Motors contain loops of wire in a magnetic field. When current is passed
> through the loops, the magnetic field exerts torque on the loops, which rotates
> a shaft. Electrical energy is converted into mechanical work in the process.
> Once the loop's surface area is aligned with the magnetic field, the direction
> of current is reversed, so there is a continual torque on the loop… This
> reversal of the current is done with commutators and brushes. The commutator is
> set to reverse the current flow at set points to keep continual motion in the
> motor."

and Figure 11.15 caption:

> "By the time the loop flips over, current flows through the wires again but now
> in the opposite direction, and the process repeats… This causes continual
> rotation of the loop."

matching the narration's "a commutator, a rotary switch that reverses the current
every half turn. Just as the loop would settle, the current flips, the torque
reverses with it, and the loop is pushed onward through another half turn… turning
the back-and-forth twist into smooth continuous rotation." ✔ (Not a "standard
application" caveat needed — it is in §11.5.) The **galvanometer** and **magnetic
potential energy** asides in the narration are standard consequences of τ = μ × B
(not in §11.5 verbatim) — marked **standard**, physically correct and consistent
with the aligning-torque result quoted above. ✔

## Problem `s4` — torque on the coil (equations `given`, `step1`, `answer`)

Self-contained application of §11.5 Eq 11.19, τ = NIAB sin θ at θ=90° (sin θ = 1),
so τ = NIAB. Numbers are our own (a small didactic coil): N=10, I=2 A, A=0.01 m²,
B=0.5 T, θ=90°. Recomputation:

```python
import math
N, I, A, B, theta = 10, 2.0, 0.01, 0.5, 90.0
tau = N*I*A*B*math.sin(math.radians(theta))
print("tau = NIAB sin(theta) = 10*2*0.01*0.5*sin90 =", tau, "N*m")
print("check expr 10*2*0.01*0.5 =", 10*2*0.01*0.5)
```

Output:

```
tau = NIAB sin(theta) = 10*2*0.01*0.5*sin90 = 0.1 N*m
check expr 10*2*0.01*0.5 = 0.1
```

Board `step1` substitutes (10)(2)(0.01)(0.5); `answer` boxes **0.1 N·m** —
matches to 0.00 %. Units: (A)(m²)(T) = A·m²·(kg/(A·s²)) = kg·m²/s² = N·m. ✔

## Problem `s5` — magnetic moment of the coil (equations `given2`, `step2`, `answer2`)

Self-contained application of §11.5 Eq 11.20, μ = NIA. Same coil: N=10, I=2 A,
A=0.01 m². Recomputation:

```python
N, I, A, B = 10, 2.0, 0.01, 0.5
mu = N*I*A
print("mu = NIA = 10*2*0.01 =", mu, "A*m^2")
print("check expr 10*2*0.01 =", 10*2*0.01)
print("cross-check tau = mu*B =", mu*B, "N*m")
```

Output:

```
mu = NIA = 10*2*0.01 = 0.2 A*m^2
check expr 10*2*0.01 = 0.2
cross-check tau = mu*B = 0.1 N*m
```

Board `step2` substitutes (10)(2)(0.01); `answer2` boxes **0.2 A·m²** — matches
to 0.00 %. Cross-check: τ = μB = 0.2·0.5 = **0.1 N·m**, identical to s4's boxed
answer, confirming τ = NIAB = (NIA)B = μB is internally consistent. Units:
(turns)(A)(m²) = A·m². ✔
