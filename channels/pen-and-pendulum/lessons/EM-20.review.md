# Fact-check — EM-20

All five equation forms match OpenStax University Physics Vol 2 §16.1 (CC BY 4.0).
The source states the four Maxwell equations in integral form (Eqs 16.8-16.11)
and the displacement-current correction (Eqs 16.2-16.4), and gives the speed of
electromagnetic waves as the speed of light, c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀). The equation bodies
render as placeholders in the extracted text, so the quoted prose and the
numbered captions in the "MAXWELL'S EQUATIONS" box are cited for each form.

## Equation `cEq`

`c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀)` — the wave speed set by the two constants of empty space.

> "In the next section, we show in more precise mathematical terms how
> Maxwell's equations lead to the prediction of electromagnetic waves that can
> travel through space without a material medium, implying a speed of
> electromagnetic waves equal to the speed of light."
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, p.662. The explicit form c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀) is derived in §16.2
> from Maxwell's equations; here §16.1 establishes that the two electromagnetic
> constants fix the wave speed and that it equals the speed of light.

## Equation `gaussE`

`∮ E·dA = Q/ε₀` — Gauss's law for electricity (Eq 16.8).

> "**Gauss's law** The electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the
> electric charge Q enclosed by the surface. Gauss's law [Equation 16.8]
> describes the relation between an electric charge and the electric field it
> produces. This is often pictured in terms of electric field lines originating
> from positive charges and terminating on negative charges…"
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, p.661, Eq 16.8.

## Equation `gaussB`

`∮ B·dA = 0` — Gauss's law for magnetism, no monopoles (Eq 16.9).

> "**Gauss's law for magnetism** The magnetic field flux through any closed
> surface is zero [Equation 16.9]. This is equivalent to the statement that
> magnetic field lines are continuous, having no beginning or end… No magnetic
> monopoles, where magnetic field lines would terminate, are known to exist."
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, pp.661-662, Eq 16.9.

## Equation `faraday`

`∮ E·dl = −dΦ_B/dt` — Faraday's law (Eq 16.10). The minus sign encodes Lenz's law.

> "**Faraday's law** A changing magnetic field induces an electromotive force
> (emf) and, hence, an electric field. The direction of the emf opposes the
> change. This third of Maxwell's equations, Equation 16.10, is Faraday's law of
> induction and includes Lenz's law. The electric field from a changing magnetic
> field has field lines that form closed loops, without any beginning or end."
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, p.661, Eq 16.10. "The direction of the emf opposes the
> change" / "includes Lenz's law" confirms the minus sign in −dΦ_B/dt.

## Equation `ampere`

`∮ B·dl = μ₀I + μ₀ε₀ dΦ_E/dt` — Ampère-Maxwell law (Eq 16.11). The second term is
Maxwell's displacement-current correction.

> "**Ampère-Maxwell law** Magnetic fields are generated by moving charges or by
> changing electric fields. This fourth of Maxwell's equations, Equation 16.11,
> encompasses Ampère's law and adds another source of magnetic fields, namely
> changing electric fields."
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, p.662, Eq 16.11.

Displacement-current correction (the μ₀ε₀ dΦ_E/dt term):

> "Maxwell discovered logical inconsistencies in these earlier results and
> identified the incompleteness of Ampère's law as their cause… Maxwell
> suggested including an additional contribution, called the displacement
> current… Here ε₀ is the permittivity of free space and Φ_E is the electric
> flux… It is produced, however, by a changing electric field. It accounts for a
> changing electric field producing a magnetic field… but the displacement
> current can produce a magnetic field even where no real current is present.
> When this extra term is included, the modified Ampère's law equation
> becomes… and is independent of the surface S through which the current I is
> measured."
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, pp.658-659, Eqs 16.2-16.4. This matches the lesson's claim
> that the μ₀ε₀ dΦ_E/dt term is Maxwell's single addition that removed the
> surface-dependence contradiction.

magnetic/permeability constant μ₀).

> "Here ε₀ is the permittivity of free space…"
> — UP Vol 2 §16.1, p.659. The numerical values are the standard SI constants
