# Fact-check — EM-19

All quotations are from **University Physics Volume 2 (OpenStax), §15.6
Transformers, CC BY 4.0** (source file
`sources/text/university-physics-vol2/135-15-6-transformers.md`, PDF pp.
645-648). Equation bodies render as placeholders in the extracted text, so the
equation body is reconstructed from the OpenStax numbering (15.20 transformer
equation, 15.21 power conservation, 15.22 current ratio) and quoted with the
surrounding prose.

## Equation `turnsEq`

`V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p`

> "This is often abbreviated as the transformer equation, which shows that the
> ratio of the secondary to primary voltages in a transformer equals the ratio
> of the number of turns in their windings." — §15.6 Transformers, Eq. 15.20, p. 647

## Equation `fluxEq`

`V = N dΦ/dt`

> "From Faraday's law, the induced potential difference is [V = N dΦ/dt] where
> [Φ] is the flux through one turn of the primary winding." — §15.6, p. 646-647

The source applies Faraday's law per coil to both the primary and the secondary
("the flux through every turn of the secondary winding is also [Φ]"), which is
exactly the "applied twice" step the lesson narrates.

## Equation `divideEq`

`V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p`

> "Combining the last two equations, we have [V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p]." — §15.6, Eq. 15.20, p. 647

Dividing the two per-coil Faraday expressions cancels the shared flux, leaving
the voltage ratio equal to the turns ratio, as the derivation states.

## Equation `powerEq`

`V_p I_p = V_s I_s`

> "From the law of energy conservation, the power introduced at any instant by
> [the source] to the primary winding must be equal to the power dissipated in
> the resistor of the secondary circuit; thus, [V_p I_p = V_s I_s]." — §15.6, Eq. 15.21, p. 647

## Equation `currentEq`

`I_s/I_p = N_p/N_s`

> "When combined with Equation 15.20, this gives [I_s/I_p = N_p/N_s]. If the
> voltage is stepped up, the current is stepped down, and vice versa." — §15.6, Eq. 15.22, p. 647

This confirms the lesson's "current ratio is the inverse of the voltage ratio"
and "double the voltage must halve the current".

**Grid needs AC (changing flux):** the source's premise is that a transformer
works by induction from a *changing* shared flux:

> "The device that transforms voltages from one value to another using
> induction is the transformer." — §15.6, p. 646

Combined with Faraday's law `V = N dΦ/dt` (above), a steady (DC) flux gives
dΦ/dt = 0 and hence zero induced voltage — supporting the lesson's claim that
the transformer needs alternating current. (The source states the induction
mechanism and the Faraday dependence on rate of change of flux; the DC-gives-
zero conclusion follows directly from dΦ/dt = 0.)
