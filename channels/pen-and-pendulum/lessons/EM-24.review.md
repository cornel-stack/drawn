## Equation `forceEq`  —  F = qv×B

> "The simplest case occurs when a charged particle moves perpendicular to a
> uniform B-field … Since the magnetic force is perpendicular to the direction
> of travel, a charged particle follows a curved path in a magnetic field."
> — UP Vol.2 §11.3. The vector Lorentz magnetic force F = qv×B is the defining
> relation of §11.1/§11.3 (Eq 11.6). Standard form, ok.

## Equation `magEq`  —  F = qvB sinθ

> "The component of the velocity perpendicular to the magnetic field produces a
> magnetic force perpendicular to both this velocity and the field … where θ is
> the angle between v and B." — UP Vol.2 §11.3, Eq 11.7. Correct magnitude form.

## Equation `perpEq`  —  F ⟂ v ⇒ W = 0

> "Another way to look at this is that the magnetic force is always
> perpendicular to velocity, so that it does no work on the charged particle.
> The particle's kinetic energy and speed thus remain constant." — UP Vol.2
> §11.3. Exactly the claim on the board.

## Equation `circleEq`  —  r = mv/qB

> "Solving for r yields [r = mv/qB]. Here, r is the radius of curvature of the
> path of a charged particle with mass m and charge q, moving at a speed v that
> is perpendicular to a magnetic field of strength B." — UP Vol.2 §11.3,
> Eq 11.4. Standard form, ok.

## Equation `wireEq`  —  F = BIL sinθ

> "If the wire section happens to be straight and B is uniform, the equation
> differentials become absolute quantities, giving us [F = IlB sinθ]. This is
> the force on a straight, current-carrying wire in a uniform magnetic field."
> — UP Vol.2 §11.4, Eq 11.13 (θ = "the angle between the magnetic field
> direction and the segment of wire"). Standard form, ok.

## Equation `torqueEq`  —  τ = NIAB sinθ

> "the net torque on the loop is … This simplifies to [τ = IAB sinθ] where
> [A] is the area of the loop. … If the loop contains N turns of wire, then its
> magnetic dipole moment is given by [μ = NIA]." — UP Vol.2 §11.5,
> Eqs 11.16–11.19. The N-turn magnitude τ = NIAB sinθ is the standard form, ok.

## Equation `biotEq`  —  dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl sinθ)/r²

> "The Biot-Savart law states that at any point P … the magnetic field due to
> an element [I dl] of a current-carrying wire is given by [dB = (μ0/4π)(I dl ×
> r̂)/r²] … The magnitude of dB is [(μ0/4π)(I dl sinθ)/r²] where θ is the angle
> between dl and r̂." — UP Vol.2 §12.1, Eqs 12.1–12.3. Standard form, ok.

## Equation `wireFieldEq`  —  B = μ₀I/2πr

> "determining the magnetic field of a long straight wire. … Substituting the
> limits gives us the solution [B = μ0 I / 2πR]. The magnetic field lines of the
> infinite wire are circular and centered at the wire." — UP Vol.2 §12.2,
> Eq 12.7. Standard form (r = R, the perpendicular distance), ok.

## Equation `ampereEq`  —  ∮ B·dl = μ₀ I_enc

> "AMPÈRE'S LAW: Over an arbitrary closed path, [∮ B·dl = μ0 I], where I is the
> total current passing through any open surface S whose perimeter is the path
> of integration. Only currents inside the path of integration need be
> considered." — UP Vol.2 §12.5, Eq 12.23. The I_enc on the board is exactly the
> "current inside the path" the text describes. Standard form, ok.

## Equation `solenoidEq`  —  B = μ₀ n I

> "the magnetic field of a solenoid … is quite uniform and directly
> proportional to the current in the wire. … The number of turns per unit
> length is N/L … the magnetic field along the central axis of an infinite
> solenoid is [B = μ0 n I]." — UP Vol.2 §12.6, Eq 12.28 (n = N/L). Standard
> form, ok.

## Equation `parallelEq`  —  F/L = μ₀ I₁ I₂ / 2πd

> "it is convenient to think in terms of F/l, the force per unit length. …
> The ratio F/l is the force per unit length between two parallel currents I1
> and I2 separated by a distance r. The force is attractive if the currents are
> in the same direction and repulsive if they are in opposite directions."
> — UP Vol.2 §12.3, Eq 12.11. On the board the separation is written d (= r in
> the source). Standard form, ok. The narration's "same direction attract,
> opposite repel" matches the quoted text exactly.

## Equation `fluxEq`  —  Φ = BA cosθ

> "The magnetic flux is a measurement of the amount of magnetic field lines
> through a given surface area … if the angle between the unit area and magnetic
> field vector are parallel … the magnetic flux is the highest possible value
> given the values of area and magnetic field." — UP Vol.2 §13.1, Eq 13.1
> [Φ = ∫ B·dA → BA cosθ for a uniform field]. Standard form, ok.

## Equation `faradayEq`  —  ε = −N dΦ/dt

> "FARADAY'S LAW: The emf induced is the negative change in the magnetic flux
> per unit time." And: "the net magnetic flux through the circuits is N times
> the flux through one turn, and Faraday's law is written as [ε = −N dΦ/dt]."
> — UP Vol.2 §13.1, Eqs 13.2–13.3. Standard form, ok.

## Equation `lenzEq`  —  induced current opposes the change

> "Lenz's law can also be considered in terms of conservation of energy. If
> pushing a magnet into a coil causes current … If the induced current causes a
> magnetic field opposing the increase in field of the magnet we pushed in, then
> the situation is clear. … If it were not the case that the induced field
> opposes the change in the flux … Electric potential energy would have been
> created, violating the conservation of energy." — UP Vol.2 §13.2. Exactly the
> statement on the board. ok.

## Equation `motionalEq`  —  ε = BLv

> "Since B and l are constant and the velocity of the rod is v, we can now
> restate Faraday's law … for the magnitude of the emf in terms of the moving
> conducting rod as [ε = Blv]." — UP Vol.2 §13.3, Eq 13.6 (L = l, the rod
> length). Standard form, ok.

## Equation `selfEq`  —  ε = −L dI/dt

> "Then Faraday's law tells us that an emf would be induced in the circuit …
> the constant of proportionality L is known as the self-inductance of the wire
> loop … the induced emf may be written in terms of the self-inductance as
> [ε = −L dI/dt]." — UP Vol.2 §14.2, Eqs 14.6–14.10. Standard form, ok.

## Equation `energyEq`  —  U = ½ L I²

> "Although derived for a special case, this equation gives the energy stored in
> the magnetic field of any inductor. … The total energy stored in the magnetic
> field when the current increases from 0 to I … can be determined by
> integrating this expression [U = ½ L I²]." — UP Vol.2 §14.3, Eq 14.22.
> Standard form, ok.

## Equation `transEq`  —  Vs/Vp = Ns/Np

> "This is often abbreviated as the transformer equation, which shows that the
> ratio of the secondary to primary voltages in a transformer equals the ratio
> of the number of turns in their windings." — UP Vol.2 §15.6, Eq 15.13
> [Vs/Vp = Ns/Np]. Standard form, ok.
