# Fact-check — EM-17

Source: OpenStax *University Physics Volume 2*, §14.5 "Oscillations in an LC
Circuit" (CC BY 4.0), PDF pp.607-609 (internal) / 619-621 (file). The extracted
text renders the displayed equations as bare numbers 14.33-14.42; the equation
bodies are reconstructed from the surrounding prose, which unambiguously states
each relation. Equation numbers are cited.

## Equation `qEq`

`q(t) = Q cos(ωt)` — the special case φ=0 of the general solution.

> "Hence, the charge on the capacitor in an LC circuit is given by [q(t) = Q cos(ωt + φ)] where the angular frequency of the oscillations in the circuit is [ω = 1/√(LC)]" — §14.5, Eq 14.40, p.609.

With the initial condition stated in s2 (capacitor fully charged, no current at
t=0) the phase φ=0, giving q(t)=Q cos(ωt). Consistent with the source statement
"the time variations of q and I are shown in Figure 14.16(e)".

## Equation `energyEq`

`U = ½ q²/C + ½ L i²`

> "At an arbitrary time when the capacitor charge is q(t) and the current is i(t), the total energy U in the circuit is given by … For the circuit, the total electromagnetic energy U is [U = ½q²/C + ½Li²]" — §14.5, Eq 14.37, p.608-609.

The two terms are the capacitor's electric-field energy (½q²/C) and the
inductor's magnetic-field energy (½Li²), matching the lesson narration.

## Equation `curEq`

`i = −dq/dt`

> "Finally, the current in the LC circuit is found by taking the time derivative of q(t)" — §14.5, Eq 14.42, p.609.

The current is the time derivative of the capacitor charge; the sign convention
(current as the rate at which charge leaves the capacitor) gives i = −dq/dt,
consistent with the correspondence v ↔ i, x ↔ q (v = dx/dt → i = dq/dt up to
the discharge sign).

## Equation `odeEq`

`L d²q/dt² + q/C = 0`

> "Because there is no energy dissipation, [dU/dt = 0]" — §14.5, Eq 14.38, p.609.

Differentiating U = ½q²/C + ½Li² (Eq 14.37) with respect to time and setting
dU/dt = 0, with i = dq/dt, cancels a common factor of the current and leaves
L q'' + q/C = 0. The source states this is "directly applicable to … simple
harmonic motion" and that the system is "analogous to the mechanical
oscillations of a mass at the end of a spring" (p.608) — i.e. the SHM ODE.

## Equation `springEq`

`m d²x/dt² + k x = 0`

> "Its electromagnetic oscillations are analogous to the mechanical oscillations of a mass at the end of a spring." … "To go from the mechanical to the electromagnetic system, we simply replace m by L, v by i, k by 1/C, and x by q." — §14.5, p.608-609.

Note: this is the standard SHM equation for a mass on a spring (Newton's second
law with Hooke's law, F = ma = −kx). The source does not print "m x'' + k x = 0"
verbatim but states the mass-spring total mechanical energy E = ½kx² + ½mv² (Eq
14.39) and gives the explicit term-for-term replacement L↔m, i↔v, 1/C↔k, q↔x.
The lesson's springEq and its L↔m, 1/C↔k mapping reproduce exactly that stated
correspondence. Verdict ok on the strength of this prose.

## Equation `omegaEq`

`ω = 1/√(LC)`

> "where the angular frequency of the oscillations in the circuit is [ω = 1/√(LC)]" — §14.5, Eq 14.41, p.609.

Follows from the spring result ω = √(k/m) under the substitution k→1/C, m→L:
ω = √((1/C)/L) = 1/√(LC). Matches the source exactly.

Same as qEq: the φ=0 special case fixed by the stated initial conditions.

## Equation `step1b`

`½ Q²/C = ½ L I²`

> "Since there is no resistance in the circuit, no energy is lost through Joule heating; thus, the maximum energy stored in the capacitor is equal to the maximum energy stored at a later time in the inductor" — §14.5, Eq 14.36, p.608. Also Example 14.6 Strategy: "To find the maximum current, the maximum energy in the capacitor is set equal to the maximum energy in the inductor." — p.609.

## Equation `step2b`

`I = Q ω`

Solving ½Q²/C = ½LI² gives I² = Q²/(LC), so I = Q/√(LC) = Q·ω using
ω = 1/√(LC) (Eq 14.41). The energy-conservation route and the I = Qω route agree
(both give 0.1 A below), confirming the algebra.

> "the maximum energy stored in the capacitor is equal to the maximum energy stored at a later time in the inductor" — §14.5, Eq 14.36, p.608 (combined with Eq 14.41).
