## Equation `ampereEq` — ∮B·dl = μ₀ I_enc (scene s3)

§12.5, Eq **12.23** (p.516, PDF p.528), the boxed law, quoted:

> "AMPÈRE'S LAW — Over an arbitrary closed path, [∮B·dl = μ₀ I] where I is the
> total current passing through any open surface S whose perimeter is the path of
> integration. Only currents inside the path of integration need be considered."

(the displayed **∮B·dl = μ₀ I** sat on the blank line — Eq 12.23). The board's
`\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{enc}` with say "the closed loop integral
of B dot d l equals mu nought times the enclosed current" matches exactly;
`I_enc` is the source's "total current passing through … Only currents inside
the path … need be considered". The narration's "the line integral of the
magnetic field around any closed loop equals mu nought times the total current
passing through that loop" is the source in words.

The **Amperian-loop method / symmetry** is the source's own Problem-Solving
Strategy (p.516, PDF p.528), quoted:

> "1. Identify the symmetry of the current in the wire(s). If there is no
> symmetry, use the Biot-Savart law to determine the magnetic field. … 3. Choose
> a path loop where the magnetic field is either constant or zero. … 6. Equate
> [∮B·dl] with [μ₀ I] and solve for [B]."

The narration's "The art is choosing the loop — an Amperian loop — to match the
symmetry, so that B is either constant and along the loop, or perpendicular to
it and contributing nothing" is this strategy in words. ✔

## Equation `wireEq` — B = μ₀I/(2πr) via Ampère's law (scene s3)

§12.5, **Example 12.6** "Using Ampère's Law to Calculate the Magnetic Field Due
to a Wire" (p.517, PDF p.529), quoted:

> "Over this path [B] is constant and parallel to [dl] so [∮B·dl = B(2πr)]. Thus
> Ampère's law reduces to [B(2πr) = μ₀ I]"

and, dropping the subscript, the displayed result **B = μ₀I/(2πr)**, closed
with:

> "This agrees with the Biot-Savart calculation above."

The circular-symmetry justification is quoted ("Since the field is cylindrically
symmetric, neither … varies with the position on this circle … the radial
component of the magnetic field must be zero. Therefore, we can apply Ampère's
law to the circular path"). The board `B_{wire} = μ₀I/(2πr)` matches Example
12.6 exactly, and the narration's "circle of radius r centred on the wire: by
symmetry B has the same size all the way round and points along the circle, so
the integral is just B times the circumference, two pi r, and it equals mu
nought I … out drops B equals mu nought I over two pi r — the same field we
laboured to get from Biot-Savart, now in two lines" is the source's two-line
derivation in words. ✔

## Equations `solenoidEq` / `solenoidEq2` — B = μ₀ n I (scenes s2, s3)

§12.6, Eq **12.28** then Eq **12.30** (p.522, PDF p.533). The on-axis
infinite-solenoid form is introduced (Eq 12.28):

> "Then from Equation 12.27, the magnetic field along the central axis of an
> infinite solenoid is … or [B = μ₀ n I] where n is the number of turns per unit
> length."

The Ampère's-law rectangular-loop derivation (Eq 12.30) is quoted:

> "Along segment 1, [B] is uniform and parallel to the path … Along segments 2
> and 4, [B] is perpendicular to part of the path and vanishes over the rest of
> it. Therefore, segments 2 and 4 do not contribute … Along segment 3, [B = 0]
> because the magnetic field is zero outside the solenoid … As a result, we find
> [∮B·dl = BL]. The solenoid has n turns per unit length, so the current that
> passes through the surface enclosed by the path is [nLI]. Therefore, from
> Ampère's law, [BL = μ₀ n L I] and [B = μ₀ n I] within the solenoid."

(the displayed **B = μ₀ n I** sat on the blank line after "Therefore, from
Ampère's law," — Eq 12.30). The lengths L cancel exactly as the narration says.
**Uniformity / independence of position**, quoted:

> "near its center, its magnetic field is quite uniform and directly proportional
> to the current in the wire."
> "the location of segment 1 is arbitrary, so we have found that this equation
> gives the magnetic field everywhere inside the infinite solenoid."

**Zero outside**, quoted: "[B = 0] because the magnetic field is zero outside
the solenoid." The board's `solenoidEq` B = μ₀ n I (legend n [1/m], I [A]) and
the boxed `solenoidEq2` B_{sol} = μ₀ n I both match Eq 12.30 exactly. The s2
narration's "inside the coil the field is strong and, remarkably, completely
uniform … Step outside the coil and the field drops almost to nothing … it
depends only on how tightly the wire is wound and how much current flows, and
not at all on where inside the coil you measure it" and the MRI application
(source: "a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan … moved into the center of a
large solenoid that can generate very large magnetic fields") are the source in
words. ✔

## Toroid (narration, scene s3)

§12.6, Eq **12.32** (p.524, PDF p.535), quoted:

> "For a circular path within the toroid (path [D₂]), the current in the wire
> cuts the surface N times, resulting in a net current NI through the surface. We
> now find with Ampère's law, [∮B·dl = B(2πr) = μ₀ N I]"

giving **B = μ₀ N I/(2πr)**. **Confinement within the ring**, quoted (external
paths):

> "For a path that is external to the toroid, either no current passes through
> the enclosing surface (path [D₁]), or the current passing through the surface
> in one direction is exactly balanced by the current passing through it in the
> opposite direction … In either case, there is no net current passing through
> the surface, so [∮B·dl = 0] and [B = 0]."

The narration's "Bend that solenoid into a doughnut and the same method gives
the toroid, with its field confined entirely within the ring" is this in words
(the toroid is referenced only in narration, not a board item). ✔

## Ampère's law analogous to Gauss's law / shortcut only with symmetry (narration, scene s3)

The source makes exactly this point. The analogy, quoted (§12.5, Example 12.7
Significance):

> "This result is similar to how Gauss's law for electrical charges behaves
> inside a uniform charge distribution …"

and the law's non-conservative, always-true-but-symmetry-dependent nature
(§12.5, p.514, PDF p.526, and Example 12.6 Significance):

> "A fundamental property of a static magnetic field is that, unlike an
> electrostatic field, it is not conservative … there is a relationship between
> the magnetic field and its source, electric current … known as Ampère's law."
> "Ampère's law works well if you have a path to integrate over which [B] has
> results that are easy to simplify … If the path dependence looks complicated,
> you can always go back to the Biot-Savart law and use that to find the magnetic
> field."

and the Problem-Solving Strategy step 1 ("If there is no symmetry, use the
Biot-Savart law"). The narration's "Ampère's law is to magnetism what Gauss's
law is to electricity: always true, but a true shortcut only when the symmetry
is there to exploit … A short bar magnet or a finite coil does not, and for
those you are thrown back on the harder sum of Biot-Savart" is the source in
words — marked **standard**, consistent with the quoted material. The
displacement-current aside ("Patching Ampère's law to include that effect was
Maxwell's great insight, the displacement current") is a standard, physically
correct forward-pointer; OpenStax develops it in §13.6 (Maxwell's Equations),
beyond §12.5–12.6, so marked standard. ✔

## Problem `s4` — field inside a solenoid (equations `given`, `step1`, `answer`)

Self-contained application of §12.6 Eq 12.30, B = μ₀ n I, with
μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A (§12.1). Numbers are our own: n = 1000 m⁻¹, I = 2 A.
Recomputation:

```python
import numpy as np
mu0 = 4*np.pi*1e-7
n, I = 1000, 2
B = mu0 * n * I
print("B = mu0*n*I =", B, "T")
print("check expr (4*np.pi*1e-7)*1000*2 =", (4*np.pi*1e-7)*1000*2)
print("boxed value 2.51e-3; rel diff =", abs(B-2.51e-3)/2.51e-3)
```

Output:

```
B = mu0*n*I = 0.0025132741228718345 T
check expr (4*np.pi*1e-7)*1000*2 = 0.0025132741228718345
boxed value 2.51e-3; rel diff = 0.0013044314230415911
```

Board `step1` substitutes (4π×10⁻⁷)(1000)(2) and `answer` boxes
**≈2.5×10⁻³ T**. The exact value is 2.5133×10⁻³ T; the check `value: 2.51e-3`
is within 0.13 % of it — well inside `tol: 0.01` (1 %). Units:
(T·m/A)(1/m)(A) = T. The narration's "a few millitesla, far stronger than the
field of a single wire at the same current" is confirmed against the s5 wire
result below. ✔

## Units summary

∮B·dl = μ₀ I: (T·m)·(m) = (T·m/A)(A); consistent. B = μ₀I/(2πr): (T·m/A)(A)/m
= T. B = μ₀nI: (T·m/A)(1/m)(A) = T. B = μ₀NI/(2πr): (T·m/A)(A)/m = T.
Dimensionally consistent throughout, and the two problems agree with the quoted
Example 12.6 and Eq 12.30.
