## Equations

### `compEq` — A = Ax i + Ay j  (component form of a vector)
§2.2 (p.54), prose immediately preceding Equation **2.12**:
> "As shown in Figure 2.16, the x- and y- components of a vector can now be
> written in terms of the unit vectors of the axes … Combining Equation 2.10
> with Equation 2.11, we obtain the component form of a vector:"
The "component form of a vector" (Eq 2.12) is exactly **A** = Ax **i** + Ay **j**,
the scalar components multiplying the axis unit vectors. The lesson item matches.
Verdict: ok.

### `magEq` — A = sqrt(Ax^2 + Ay^2)  (magnitude via Pythagoras)
§2.2 (p.56), prose labelling Equation **2.15**:
> "Because the lengths A, [Ax], and [Ay] form a right triangle, they are related
> by the Pythagorean theorem:"
The Pythagorean relation for the right triangle of the two components gives
A = sqrt(Ax^2 + Ay^2) (Eq 2.15; generalised to three dimensions as Eq 2.21,
p.61). The lesson's two-dimensional form matches. Verdict: ok.

### `dirEq` — tan(theta) = Ay / Ax  (direction angle)
§2.2 (p.56), prose labelling Equation **2.16**:
> "The direction angle [theta] of a vector is defined via the tangent function
> of angle [theta] in the triangle shown in Figure 2.18:"
This is tan(theta) = Ay / Ax (Eq 2.16), confirmed by the companion statement
(p.58) that "the quotient of the opposite side [Ay] to the hypotenuse A is the
sine function" and the adjacent side gives the cosine, i.e. the same right
triangle. The lesson item matches. Verdict: ok.

### `sumEq` — C = A + B  (resultant; add component by component)
§2.3 (p.64), prose labelling Equations **2.24** and **2.25**:
> "to find the resultant of two vectors A and B, we simply add them component by
> component … scalar components of the resultant vector [C] are the sums of
> corresponding scalar components of vectors A and B:"
The resultant **C** = **A** + **B** is formed by Cx = Ax + Bx, Cy = Ay + By
(Eq 2.25), the component statement of the tip-to-tail / parallelogram rule the
lesson draws. Verdict: ok.

### `dotEq` — A·B = AB cos(theta) = AxBx + AyBy  (dot product)
§2.4 (p.72), definition box "SCALAR PRODUCT (DOT PRODUCT)", Equation **2.27**:
> "The scalar product [A·B] of two vectors A and B is a number defined by the
> equation … where [theta] is the angle between the vectors (shown in Figure
> 2.27)."
That defining equation is A·B = AB cos(theta) (Eq 2.27). The component form is
§2.4 (p.73), prose labelling Equation **2.33**:
> "the scalar product simplifies to …"
which is A·B = AxBx + AyBy + AzBz (Eq 2.33); the lesson's two-dimensional
A·B = AxBx + AyBy is this with Az = Bz = 0. The projection interpretation the
lesson narrates ("the shaded stretch is the projection … multiply that
projection by the first vector's length") matches Eq 2.31 (p.73): the dot product
"can also be interpreted as … the product of B with the projection of A onto the
direction of B". Verdict: ok.

### `crossEq` — |A×B| = AB sin(theta)  (magnitude of the cross product)
§2.4 (p.76), definition box "VECTOR PRODUCT (CROSS PRODUCT)", Equation **2.35**:
> "The magnitude of the vector product is defined as … where angle [theta],
> between the two vectors, is measured from vector A (first vector in the
> product) to vector B (second vector in the product) … and is between [0] and
> [pi]."
That defining magnitude is |A×B| = AB sin(theta) (Eq 2.35). The lesson's
supporting narration is also sourced: the result is "a vector that has its
direction perpendicular to both vectors A and B" (p.76), the right-hand rule
("position your right hand so that your fingers point in the direction of A and
they curl toward B … Your thumb now points toward the correct choice", p.77),
anticommutativity ("the vector product reverses the sign when the order of
multiplication is reversed", Eq 2.36, p.77), and vanishing for parallel vectors
("the vector product vanishes for pairs of vectors that are either parallel or
antiparallel because sin = 0", p.76). The lesson's "area of the parallelogram
they span" is the standard geometric reading of AB sin(theta). Verdict: ok.

by reading `channels/pen-and-pendulum/lessons/ME-01.yaml` (no `check:` key, no
`role:` board items). The only numeric claim in the whole lesson is the
quick 3-4-5 example in the s3 narration:

> "components of three across and four up give a length of exactly five, since
> nine plus sixteen is twenty-five."

Recomputation (Python):

```python
import math
Ax, Ay = 3, 4
print('Ax^2 + Ay^2 =', Ax**2 + Ay**2)
print('sqrt(Ax^2+Ay^2) =', math.sqrt(Ax**2 + Ay**2))
```

Printed output:

```
Ax^2 + Ay^2 = 25
sqrt(Ax^2+Ay^2) = 5.0
```

9 + 16 = 25 and sqrt(25) = 5.0 exactly, so the narration's "nine plus sixteen is
twenty-five" and "a length of exactly five" are both correct. This is an
application of `magEq` (Eq 2.15). Verdict: ok.
