# Fact-check — EM-13 (Faraday's law and Lenz's law: electromagnetic induction)

Sources: OpenStax *University Physics Volume 2* (CC BY 4.0), §13.1 *Faraday's
Law* (book p.546–549, PDF p.558–561) and §13.2 *Lenz's Law* (book p.550–553,
PDF p.562–565). Extracted text:
`sources/text/university-physics-vol2/111-13-1-faraday-s-law.md`,
`112-13-2-lenz-s-law.md`.

> as images, so the symbolic glyphs are absent from the extracted text — only

## Equation `fluxEq` — Φ = B A cosθ (scene s3)

§13.1, Eq **13.1** (p.547, PDF p.559), quoted:

> "The magnetic flux is a measurement of the amount of magnetic field lines
> through a given surface area, as seen in Figure 13.4. This definition is
> similar to the electric flux studied earlier. This means that if we have
> [Φ = ∫ B·dA] …"

(the displayed **Φ = ∫B·dA** sat on the blank line after "if we have" —
Eq 13.1). The square-on / angle dependence is in the Fig 13.4 caption:

> "If the angle between the unit area [n̂] and magnetic field vector [B] are
> parallel or antiparallel, as shown in the diagram, the magnetic flux is the
> highest possible value given the values of area and magnetic field."

The board's `Φ = B A cosθ` is the uniform-field, flat-surface reduction of the
general integral (∫B·dA → BA cosθ for uniform B over a planar area A whose
normal makes angle θ with B), exactly the step Example 13.1 Strategy performs:
"We will choose this [n̂] to be pointing downward so that [n̂] is parallel to
[B] and that the flux turns into multiplication of magnetic field times area."
The SI unit is quoted ("The SI unit for magnetic flux is the weber (Wb)") —
matches the legend. The narration's "field times the area it passes through,
when the field is square-on — more generally, field times area times the cosine
of the angle between them" is the source in words. ✔

## Equation `faradayEq` — ε = −dΦ/dt (scene s2)

§13.1, Eq **13.2**, the boxed law (p.547, PDF p.559), quoted:

> "FARADAY'S LAW — The emf [ε] induced is the negative change in the magnetic
> flux [Φ] per unit time. Any change in the magnetic field or change in
> orientation of the area of the coil with respect to the magnetic field induces
> a voltage (emf)."

(the displayed **ε = −dΦ/dt** sat on the blank line — Eq 13.2; the body text
"then the induced emf or the voltage generated by a conductor or coil moving in
a magnetic field is" precedes it). The negative-sign note is quoted: "The
negative sign describes the direction in which the induced emf drives current
around a circuit. However, that direction is most easily determined with a rule
known as Lenz's law." The board's `ε = −dΦ/dt` (legend ε [V], Φ [Wb]) matches
Eq 13.2 exactly. The s2 narration's "the induced voltage is the rate at which
this flux changes … a flux that is large but unchanging … induces no voltage at
all, while a flux that changes quickly … induces a large one … It is never the
amount of flux that matters, only how fast it changes" is Faraday's law
(rate-of-change, not amount) in words, consistent with the experimental summary
"The faster the motion, the greater the emf, and there is no emf when the magnet
is stationary relative to the coil." ✔

## Equation `faradayEq2` — ε = −N dΦ/dt (scene s3)

§13.1, Eq **13.3** (p.548, PDF p.560), quoted:

> "In many practical applications, the circuit of interest consists of a number
> N of tightly wound turns (see Figure 13.6). Each turn experiences the same
> magnetic flux. Therefore, the net magnetic flux through the circuits is N times
> the flux through one turn, and Faraday's law is written as [ε = −N dΦ/dt]."

(the displayed **ε = −N dΦ/dt** sat on the blank line after "written as" —
Eq 13.3). The board's boxed `ε = −N dΦ/dt` matches Eq 13.3 exactly, and the
narration's "for a coil of N turns it is N times larger, since each turn is
threaded in its own right" is this verbatim in words. The **three ways to change
the flux** is Eq 13.2's own clause: "Any change in the magnetic field or change
in orientation of the area of the coil with respect to the magnetic field
induces a voltage" — the narration's "change the field's strength, change the
loop's area, or rotate the loop to change the angle" expands "orientation of the
area" into the area-and-angle pair, consistent with Φ = BA cosθ. ✔

## Equation `lenzEq` — induced current opposes the change (scene s3)

§13.2, the boxed **LENZ'S LAW** (p.550, PDF p.562), quoted:

> "LENZ'S LAW — The direction of the induced emf drives current around a wire
> loop to always oppose the change in magnetic flux that causes the emf."

The **conservation-of-energy** basis is quoted (§13.2, p.550):

> "Lenz's law can also be considered in terms of conservation of energy. If
> pushing a magnet into a coil causes current, the energy in that current must
> have come from somewhere … We pushed a magnet against a field and did work on
> the system, and that showed up as current. If it were not the case that the
> induced field opposes the change in the flux, the magnet would be pulled in and
> produce a current without any work. Electric potential energy would have been
> created, violating the conservation of energy."

The board's text item `induced current opposes the change` (the physical meaning
of the minus sign) matches the boxed Lenz's law exactly. The narration's "The
induced current always flows in the direction whose own magnetic field opposes
the change in flux that produced it … If the induced current reinforced the
change instead of opposing it … you would have unlimited energy from nothing — a
perpetual-motion machine" is the quoted energy argument in words, consistent
with the §13.2 worked wording "the direction of the induced current must be such
that its own magnetic field is directed in a way to oppose the changing flux."
The "resistance when you push a magnet into a coil" / generator drag is the
source's "We pushed a magnet against a field and did work on the system." ✔

## Applications — generators, transformers (narration, scene s3)

The source establishes the two-circuit (transformer) effect explicitly (§13.1,
p.546, PDF p.558):

> "Faraday also discovered that a similar effect can be produced using two
> circuits—a changing current in one circuit induces a current in a second,
> nearby circuit."

The generator principle (spinning a coil so its flux rises and falls, inducing
an emf) is the direct application of Eq 13.2's "change in orientation of the area
of the coil with respect to the magnetic field induces a voltage." The specific
devices named in narration — power-station generators, transformers, induction
cooktops, tap-to-pay cards, wireless chargers, guitar pickups — are standard,
physically correct applications of Faraday's/Lenz's law; OpenStax develops
generators and transformers in §13.4 and §15.5, beyond §13.1–13.2, so these are
marked **standard**, consistent with the two-circuit quote above. ✔

## Problem `s4` — EMF from a changing field (equations `given`, `step1`, `answer`)

Self-contained application of §13.1 Faraday's law, with the area factored out of
the time derivative (Example 13.1 Strategy: "The area of the loop is not changing
in time, so it can be factored out of the time derivative, leaving the magnetic
field as the only quantity varying in time"), so ε = N A dB/dt. Numbers are our
own: A = 0.02 m², dB/dt = 3 T/s, N = 1. Recomputation:

```python
A = 0.02
dBdt = 3
N = 1
emf = N * A * dBdt
print("EMF = N*A*dB/dt =", emf, "V")
print("check expr 0.02*3 =", 0.02*3)
print("boxed value 0.06; rel diff =", abs(emf-0.06)/0.06)
```

Output:

```
EMF = N*A*dB/dt = 0.06 V
check expr 0.02*3 = 0.06
boxed value 0.06; rel diff = 0.0
```

Board `step1` substitutes (0.02)(3) and `answer` boxes **0.06 V** (= 60 mV,
matching the narration "sixty millivolts"). Matches the boxed value exactly
(rel diff 0.0). Units: (m²)(T/s) = T·m²/s = Wb/s = V. The narration's "Double the
rate at which the field grows and you double the voltage; let the field settle to
a steady value and the voltage falls to zero" is the ∝ dB/dt dependence of
Eq 13.2. ✔

## Problem `s5` — magnetic flux through a loop (equations `given2`, `step2`, `answer2`)

Self-contained application of §13.1 Eq 13.1 flux definition at θ = 0 (cos0 = 1),
exactly as Example 13.2 Solution writes it ("Since [B] is perpendicular to the
plane of the coil, the magnetic flux is given by [Φ = BA]"). Numbers are our own:
B = 0.5 T, A = 0.02 m², θ = 0. Recomputation:

```python
import math
B = 0.5
A = 0.02
theta = 0
Phi = B * A * math.cos(theta)
print("Phi = B*A*cos0 =", Phi, "Wb")
print("check expr 0.5*0.02 =", 0.5*0.02)
print("boxed value 0.01; rel diff =", abs(Phi-0.01)/0.01)
```

Output:

```
Phi = B*A*cos0 = 0.01 Wb
check expr 0.5*0.02 = 0.01
boxed value 0.01; rel diff = 0.0
```

Board `step2` substitutes (0.5)(0.02) and `answer2` boxes **0.01 Wb**. Matches
the boxed value exactly (rel diff 0.0). Units: (T)(m²) = T·m² = Wb (§13.1: "The
SI unit for magnetic flux is the weber (Wb)"). The narration's "This flux, held
steady, induces no voltage — remember, only its change does" is Eq 13.2's
rate-of-change dependence. ✔
